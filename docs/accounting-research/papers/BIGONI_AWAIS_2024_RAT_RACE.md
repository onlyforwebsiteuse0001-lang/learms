# Deep Analysis — Bigoni & Awais (2025), "I am no longer part of this rat race"

**Area:** 12 (research papers)
**Status:** The single most important academic source located in this research.
**Why it gets its own file:** it is the only peer-reviewed, primary-evidence study found that
documents what Pakistani accounting students actually experience — and its findings directly
contradict assumptions embedded in the Learms brief.

---

## 1. Full citation

> Bigoni, M. and Awais, M. (2025) '"I am no longer part of this rat race": accounting education
> and students' resistance in Pakistan', *Accounting Forum*, 49(2), pp. 472–497.
> DOI: [10.1080/01559982.2024.2309595](https://doi.org/10.1080/01559982.2024.2309595)

- Published online 2024; assigned to issue 49(2), 2025.
- Open-access Version of Record: https://shura.shu.ac.uk/33485/3/Awais-IAmNoLongerPart(VoR).pdf
  (Sheffield Hallam University Research Archive, accessed 2026-09-30)
- *Accounting Forum* is an ABS/AJG-ranked interdisciplinary accounting journal.

**Method:** qualitative; **≥20 semi-structured interviews** with accounting students across
**five Pakistani universities**, recruited by snowball sampling.
**Theory:** James C. Scott's concept of **infrapolitics** — low-visibility, everyday resistance by
subordinate groups who cannot afford open confrontation.

---

## 2. Core argument

Pakistani accounting education transmits a curriculum built around **Anglo-American corporate
reality** to students who will work in a **Pakistani economy of family firms, jewellers,
restaurants, textile units and informal trade**. Students perceive the mismatch clearly. They
cannot openly challenge it — institutional power is asymmetric and the credential is their only
asset — so resistance goes underground: disengagement, instrumental cramming, private cynicism,
and eventually exit.

The title comes from Student 11's account of leaving: **"I am no longer part of this rat race."**

---

## 3. The evidence, in students' own words

| Quote (verbatim) | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| Students describe themselves as **"robots"** and **"beasts of burden"** | Dehumanisation through volume-based rote learning |
| **"Enron Enron Enron. I am tired of this name"** | Curriculum recycles 2001-vintage American cases as the ethics canon |
| **"we [have] data from Apple and Microsoft but neither of them is in Pakistan… we should look at local jeweller shops or restaurants"** | Students independently diagnose the relevance gap and can articulate the fix |
| **"I am no longer part of this rat race"** (Student 11) | Exit as the terminal form of resistance |

**The Enron quote deserves emphasis.** Enron collapsed in 2001. A student in the mid-2020s is
being taught corporate ethics through a case that predates their birth, set in a market they will
never work in, while Pakistan's own corporate governance failures go untaught. That is not a
curriculum lag — it is a statement about whose reality counts.

## 4. Why this matters for Learms — six direct implications

### 4.1 The most-demanded missing content is *local context*
This is the paper's clearest actionable finding. Students do not ask for easier material or more
videos. They ask for **Pakistani examples**. A platform that presents variance analysis using a
Faisalabad textile unit, working capital using a Karachi trading firm, and ethics using a
documented Pakistani case is addressing the top-ranked student grievance in the only peer-reviewed
study of this population.

**This should be a content authoring rule in Learms, not an aspiration.** Recommend Agent 2 treat
"has a Pakistani worked example" as a required field on explanatory content, with a visible gap
report for concepts that lack one.

### 4.2 Disengagement is *rational*, not a motivation deficit
Standard ed-tech treats dropout as a motivation problem and answers with streaks, badges and
nudges. This paper reframes it: students disengage because they have correctly judged the content
to be irrelevant to their working lives. **Gamifying irrelevant content is answering the wrong
question** — and students who describe themselves as "robots" are unlikely to respond well to
being further instrumented.

**Implication for Agent 1:** a low-engagement signal should not automatically be modelled as low
mastery or low motivation. It may be a content-relevance signal. Consider capturing
*why* a student disengaged rather than only *that* they did.

### 4.3 Resistance is invisible by design — so surveys will miss it
Infrapolitics means students deliberately avoid open complaint. **A satisfaction survey will not
find this.** Any Learms feedback mechanism that requires attributable, visible criticism will
systematically under-detect the problem it most needs to detect.

**Implication for Agent 3 (privacy):** anonymous, non-attributable feedback is not a
nice-to-have here; it is the only channel through which this category of signal can surface. This
is a research-grounded privacy requirement, not a generic one.

### 4.4 It corroborates the rote-learning finding from an independent direction
The ICMAP exam structure (heavy MCQ, fixed-format papers) and the interview evidence ("robots")
point at the same phenomenon from opposite ends — institutional design and lived experience.
Neither alone would be conclusive; together they are strong.

### 4.5 Students are competent diagnosticians
The jeweller/restaurant quote is a student proposing a specific, sound pedagogical reform. Learms
should treat students as a source of curriculum intelligence, not only as consumers of it.

### 4.6 Caveats — stated honestly
- **Universities, not professional institutes.** The study covers university accounting students.
  ICMAP/ICAP/ACCA students overlap heavily with this population but are not identical. Extending
  the findings to professional-body students is **inference, not evidence** — labelled as such
  wherever used in this research.
- **≥20 interviews, five universities, snowball sampling.** Qualitative and non-representative by
  design. It establishes that these experiences exist and are articulate; it does **not**
  establish prevalence. No percentage should ever be attached to these findings.
- Single study. No replication located.

## 5. Contradiction with the brief

The Learms brief frames Pakistani accounting students' problems as predominantly **financial and
logistical** (fees, materials, coaching access). This paper's evidence says the deepest problem is
**epistemic**: the content itself is felt to be foreign and pointless.

These are not mutually exclusive, but they imply very different products. A platform built only
on the brief's premise would deliver cheaper access to the same alienating material. Logged as a
standing tension in `../CONTRADICTIONS.md`.

## 6. Search-hygiene warning attached to this literature

Searching for **"ICMAP"** in academic databases returns *"ICMAP Students' Problems and Solutions
in Learning English"* (IntechOpen / ResearchGate, 2024), which concerns Indonesia's **International
Class of Management and Accounting Program** — **not** the Institute of Cost and Management
Accountants of Pakistan. Any automated literature ingestion in Learms will pull this in and it
will look plausibly on-topic.

**Recommendation for Agent 1:** add an acronym-disambiguation rule to the search index. This is a
concrete, already-observed false positive, not a hypothetical.
