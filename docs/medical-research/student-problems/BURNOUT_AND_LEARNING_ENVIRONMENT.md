# Burnout and the medical-school learning environment

## Topic
Global burnout estimate depends on definition and instrument

### Source
Frajerman et al. systematic review/meta-analysis of 24 studies and 17,431 pre-residency medical students. [MED-089]

### Key Finding
Estimated current burnout prevalence was 44.2% (95% CI 33.4–55.0); emotional exhaustion 40.8%, depersonalization 35.1%, and low personal accomplishment 27.4% in the subset reporting dimensions. Burnout measurement and cut-offs differed among studies, and the wide confidence interval cautions against treating 44.2% as universal.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not reduce burnout to missed study goals or screen-time patterns. Let learners describe workload, recovery and support needs without displaying an unvalidated “burnout score.”

### Citation
Frajerman A, Morvan Y, Krebs MO, Gorwood P, Chaumette B. (2019). Burnout in medical students before residency: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *European Psychiatry*, 55, 36–42. doi:10.1016/j.eurpsy.2018.08.006.

## Topic
Pakistan prevalence estimates conflict because constructs and instruments differ

### Source
Cross-sectional studies by Asghar et al., Irshad et al., Baqai et al., and Shahzad & Wajid. [MED-096, MED-100, MED-107, MED-108]

### Key Finding
The studies cannot be placed on one trend line: Asghar et al. reported 18.2% meeting its MBI-HSS burnout definition among 600 Karachi students; Baqai et al. reported 38% high total burnout and 80% academic burnout using BAT-23 among 392 medical/dental students; Shahzad & Wajid reported dimension-specific single-item results among 3,400 Lahore students; Irshad et al. used BCSQ-12 and emphasized associations with low household income and sleep deprivation. Different samples, tools, dimensions and thresholds explain why a single “Pakistan burnout rate” is unsupported.

### Relevance to Learms
Store instrument, version, cutoff, dimension, population and study date. Never compare scores across instruments or infer a national change from cross-sectional studies.

### Citation
Asghar AA et al. (2019), *Cureus* 11(6):e4879, doi:10.7759/cureus.4879. Irshad K et al. (2022), *Pakistan Journal of Medical Sciences* 38(4):801–806, doi:10.12669/pjms.38.4.5052. Baqai S et al. (2024), *Journal of the College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan*, doi:10.29271/jcpsp.2024.12.1508. Shahzad S, Wajid G. (2024), *BMJ Open* 14:e080440, doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2023-080440.

## Topic
Learning environment and wellbeing are associated, not proven causal

### Source
Shahzad & Wajid cross-sectional study of 3,400 students at six randomly selected public/private medical colleges in Lahore using JHLES, quality-of-life and burnout items. [MED-100]

### Key Finding
Mean JHLES score was 81.7±13.5. The study found year/residential-status relationships with emotional exhaustion and complex relationships between learning-environment scores, quality of life and depersonalization. Its cross-sectional design cannot show whether learning environment caused the outcomes, and Lahore results do not represent all Pakistan colleges.

### Relevance to Learms
A platform may collect separate, optional feedback about workload clarity, belonging, supervision and recovery. It should not publish college league tables from non-comparable self-report samples.

### Citation
Shahzad S, Wajid G. (2024). Learning environment and its relationship with quality of life and burn-out among undergraduate medical students in Pakistan: a cross-sectional study. *BMJ Open*, 14(8), e080440. doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2023-080440.

## Topic
Recent student narratives locate burnout in systems as well as individuals

### Source
Khurshid, Khurshid & Toor qualitative study: one focus group with 12 purposively selected students in Rawalpindi/Islamabad who self-reported burnout experiences. [MED-095]

### Key Finding
Themes included academic pressure, exhausting clinical demands, emotional distress, weak or distrusted support, family/financial pressure, social comparison and career uncertainty. Participants requested accessible stigma-free care, mentoring, peer support, workload reform and clearer career guidance. The sample was deliberately small and selected for relevant experience; themes show depth, not prevalence.

### Relevance to Learms
Avoid framing burnout as a learner's failure of resilience. Product support should include workload and institutional routes, while intervention effectiveness must be evaluated separately in Area 5.

### Citation
Khurshid S, Khurshid S, Toor HK. (2025). Burnout as a mental health challenge among medical students in Pakistan: a qualitative study of its triggers, impacts, and support needs. *BMC Medical Education*, 25, 1190. doi:10.1186/s12909-025-07762-y.

## Topic
Regulatory standards include counselling and wellbeing

### Source
PM&DC 2024 undergraduate guidance and medical-college accreditation standard. [MED-001, MED-106]

### Key Finding
The guidance calls for accessible, confidential academic, social, psychological and financial support and career guidance. The accreditation standard requires psychological, academic and career counselling and adequate wellbeing mechanisms. These standards support institution-level provision, not just self-care content.

### Relevance to Learms
Institution profiles may show dated, verified support-service fields. “Available” should require institution-provided evidence and a verification date; presence of a policy must not be represented as service quality or utilization.

### Citation
Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (2024). *Guidelines for Undergraduate Medical Education MBBS Curriculum*. Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (2024). *Accreditation Standards for Medical College up to 100 MBBS Students*. Official PM&DC PDFs, accessed 2026-09-30.
