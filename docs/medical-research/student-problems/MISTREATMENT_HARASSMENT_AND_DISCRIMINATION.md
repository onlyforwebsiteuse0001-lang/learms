# Mistreatment, harassment and discrimination

## Topic
Global prevalence and source patterns

### Source
Fnais et al. systematic review/meta-analysis of 51 studies. [MED-091]

### Key Finding
59.4% (95% CI 52.0–66.7) of medical trainees reported at least one form of harassment or discrimination; verbal harassment was the most frequently reported form. Consultants were the most frequently cited source, followed by patients or patients' families. Definitions, trainee levels and settings varied, so the estimate is a broad signal rather than a college-specific benchmark.

### Relevance to Learms
Mistreatment must not be reduced to a generic “stress” tag. Support routes should distinguish immediate safety, institutional reporting, confidential advice, documentation, and wellbeing support.

### Citation
Fnais N, Soobiah C, Chen MH, et al. (2014). Harassment and Discrimination in Medical Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. *Academic Medicine*, 89(5), 817–827. doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000000200.

## Topic
Multicollege Pakistan bullying survey

### Source
Ahmer et al. cross-sectional survey of final-year students in six Pakistan medical colleges. [MED-097]

### Key Finding
52% reported bullying or harassment during medical education; about 28% of affected respondents experienced it monthly or more often. Verbal abuse was the dominant reported form and consultants were the most frequent reported perpetrators. Lack of a policy and inadequate perceived support were associated with reporting bullying. The 2008 data are historical and final-year/self-report results are not a current national prevalence.

### Relevance to Learms
A learner-facing directory should expose the institution's current policy, inquiry committee route, independent escalation route and last verification date—without claiming that policy presence proves safety.

### Citation
Ahmer S, Yousafzai AW, Bhutto N, Alam S, Sarangzai AK, Iqbal A. (2008). Bullying of Medical Students in Pakistan: A Cross-Sectional Questionnaire Survey. *PLOS ONE*, 3(12), e3889. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0003889.

## Topic
Single-institution Pakistan mistreatment study

### Source
Shoukat et al. cross-sectional survey of 232 respondents at a private Karachi medical school. [MED-098]

### Key Finding
62.5% reported mistreatment; clinical faculty were the most commonly reported source. The survey covered belittlement, yelling, punitive tasks, unfair grading threats, physical acts, sexual harassment and discrimination. The study did not find a statistically significant association between mistreatment and psychiatric morbidity in its sample; that null result does not show mistreatment is harmless.

### Relevance to Learms
Keep incident type and requested support separate. Do not require a mental-health symptom report before allowing harassment support or reporting information.

### Citation
Shoukat S, Anis M, Kella DK, et al. (2010). Prevalence of Mistreatment or Belittlement among Medical Students—A Cross Sectional Survey at a Private Medical School in Karachi, Pakistan. *PLOS ONE*, 5(10), e13429. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0013429.

## Topic
Pakistan HEI policy establishes formal institutional routes

### Source
Higher Education Commission Pakistan revised harassment policy. [MED-105]

### Key Finding
The policy prohibits harassment in Pakistan HEIs, extends protections across the HEI community, describes reporting to an inquiry committee, requires confidentiality, sets committee composition and procedural timeframes, and addresses prevention education. It also covers certain off-campus conduct connected to HEI programs. Product summaries must link to the current source and must not replace institutional/legal advice.

### Relevance to Learms
A report-help screen should be jurisdiction- and institution-specific. It should clearly state whether Learms merely links out, transmits a report, or stores notes; each mode has different privacy and safeguarding consequences.

### Citation
Higher Education Commission Pakistan (n.d.). *Revised Policy on Protection against Harassment in Higher Education Institutions*. https://www.hec.gov.pk/english/services/Documents/HARASSMENT-POLICY.pdf (accessed 2026-09-30).

## Topic
Privacy and retaliation-sensitive design

### Source
HEC policy confidentiality requirements plus Pakistan studies documenting hierarchical sources. [MED-097, MED-098, MED-105]

### Key Finding
Where alleged perpetrators may assess or supervise students, a simple “tell your supervisor” flow can reproduce the power conflict. HEC requires formal procedures and confidentiality, while the studies identify senior clinicians/faculty as common reported sources. The evidence does not establish that any one reporting channel is trusted or effective at every institution.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not create an unmandated complaint repository. If notes are offered, default to local/private storage where feasible, warn about subpoena/institutional-access limits, minimize identifiers, and provide independent as well as institutional routes after verification by Agent 3/legal review.

### Citation
Ahmer et al. (2008), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0003889. Shoukat et al. (2010), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0013429. Higher Education Commission Pakistan, revised harassment policy, accessed 2026-09-30.

## Evidence gap

Recent representative national data by gender, disability, religion/ethnicity, socioeconomic status, sexual orientation, program, clinical site, and reporting outcome were **not found**. Evidence that a complaint was filed must not be treated as evidence of guilt; support and adjudication are separate functions.
