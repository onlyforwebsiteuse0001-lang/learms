## 2. Structure — 13 exams, three levels

| Level | Exams | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Applied Knowledge** (3) | Business and Technology (BT), Management Accounting (MA), Financial Accounting (FA) | On-demand CBE, sat any time |
| **Applied Skills** (6) | Corporate and Business Law (LW), Performance Management (PM), Taxation (TX), Financial Reporting (FR), Audit and Assurance (AA), Financial Management (FM) | Session CBE (LW on-demand in some markets) |
| **Strategic Professional — Essentials** (2) | Strategic Business Leader (SBL), Strategic Business Reporting (SBR) | Both compulsory |
| **Strategic Professional — Options** (2 of 4) | Advanced Financial Management (AFM), Advanced Performance Management (APM), Advanced Taxation (ATX), Advanced Audit and Assurance (AAA) | Choose any two |

Plus two non-exam requirements:
- **Ethics and Professional Skills Module (EPSM)** — to be started after Applied Knowledge and
  before Strategic Professional.
- **Practical Experience Requirement (PER)** — 36 months of relevant supervised experience.

Four exam sessions per year: **March, June, September, December**.

## 3. Fees — official, GBP, identical in Pakistan

**Confirmed:** ACCA's Pakistan fee page publishes the same GBP amounts as its global pages.
ACCA does not discount for Pakistani purchasing power. This is the single most important
economic fact about ACCA in Pakistan and it drives almost every affordability complaint.

| Item | GBP |
|---|---|
| Initial registration (one-time) | **89** |
| Re-registration (if lapsed) | **89** |
| Annual subscription 2026 | **140** |
| Exemption fee — Applied Knowledge, per paper | **86** |
| Exemption fee — Applied Skills, per paper | **114** |
| Applied Knowledge exam | **set by the local CBE centre** — not fixed by ACCA |
| Applied Skills exam — Dec 2026 standard entry | **160** |
| Applied Skills exam — Dec 2026 **late** entry | **409** |
| Ethics and Professional Skills Module | **83** |
| Strategic Business Leader — standard / late | **282 / 467** |
| Strategic Business Reporting — standard / late | **208 / 467** |
| Options (AFM/APM/ATX/AAA) — standard / late | **208 / 467** |

### 3.1 The late-entry penalty is the most expensive avoidable mistake in ACCA

| Paper type | Standard | Late | Penalty | % increase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Applied Skills | £160 | £409 | **+£249** | **+156%** |
| SBL | £282 | £467 | +£185 | +66% |
| SBR / Options | £208 | £467 | **+£259** | **+125%** |

The standard-entry window for December 2026 closed **2 November 2026**; late entry closed
**9 November 2026**. **Seven days of delay more than doubles the cost of an Applied Skills
paper.**

**This is the highest-value, lowest-effort feature Learms could build for ACCA students:** a
hard deadline alarm tied to each session's standard-entry close date. It is a pure
information problem with a large, quantified financial consequence. Flagged to Agent 2 in
`../RECOMMENDATIONS.md`.

### 3.2 Total cost modelled (Tier E — my arithmetic on Tier A fees)

Assumes standard entry throughout, no resits, no lapses, 3 years to complete.
Applied Knowledge exam fees excluded because ACCA does not set them (CBE centres do).

**Route A — no exemptions, all 13 papers:**

| Component | GBP |
|---|---|
| Initial registration | 89 |
| Annual subscription × 3 | 420 |
| Applied Skills 6 × 160 | 960 |
| SBL | 282 |
| SBR | 208 |
| Options 2 × 208 | 416 |
| EPSM | 83 |
| **Subtotal (excl. Applied Knowledge exams)** | **2,458** |

**Route B — Pakistani B.Com/BBA graduate with 4 exemptions** (3 Applied Knowledge + 1 Applied
Skills is a common profile; actual awards vary by institution and are determined by ACCA):

| Component | GBP |
|---|---|
| Initial registration | 89 |
| Annual subscription × 3 | 420 |
| Exemption fees: 3 × 86 + 1 × 114 | 372 |
| Applied Skills 5 × 160 | 800 |
| SBL + SBR + 2 Options | 906 |
| EPSM | 83 |
| **Subtotal** | **2,670** |

**Note the trap:** exemptions do **not** save money at 4 papers. £372 in exemption fees versus
£258 + £160 = £418 to sit three Applied Knowledge exams (at a typical centre price) and one
Applied Skills paper. Exemptions save *time and risk*, not cash, and at Applied Skills level
(£114 exemption vs £160 exam) the saving is only £46 per paper — while forfeiting the
foundational study. **Learms should present exemptions as a time/risk decision, not a
cost-saving one.** Many Pakistani students assume the opposite.

### 3.3 Converting to PKR

ACCA's fees are GBP-denominated and therefore **rise in PKR terms whenever the rupee
depreciates, independently of any ACCA price rise.** A student who budgets in rupees at
enrolment faces currency risk over a 3-year qualification that they have no ability to hedge.

⚠️ **An authoritative GBP/PKR rate for the access date was not captured in this session**, so
no PKR total is asserted here. The correct way for Learms to present this is a **live
conversion at the current interbank rate**, not a hard-coded figure — because a hard-coded
figure is wrong within weeks. See `../BLOCKERS-7.md` B-011.

**Structural finding for the comparison work:** ICMAP and ICAP price in PKR; ACCA prices in
GBP. Over a multi-year qualification during a period of rupee depreciation, ACCA's *real*
cost to a Pakistani student rises faster than either domestic body's, even if ACCA never
raises a single fee. This is a materially different risk profile that comparison tables
essentially never show.

## 4. Pass rates — ACCA publishes them, and they are the best difficulty data in the sector

**Pass mark is fixed at 50% for every ACCA exam.** The pass *rate* is the share of candidates
reaching it and varies by paper and session.

### Applied Knowledge and Applied Skills (%)

| Session | BT | MA | FA | LW | PM | TX | FR | AA | FM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2026 | – | – | – | – | 45 | 53 | 50 | 43 | 50 |
| Dec 2025 | 87 | 64 | 68 | 82 | 40 | 55 | 51 | 46 | 48 |
| Sep 2025 | – | – | – | – | 43 | 55 | 48 | 46 | 46 |
| Jun 2025 | 88 | 64 | 68 | 81 | 43 | 54 | 50 | 44 | 48 |

(BT, MA, FA and LW are on-demand exams, reported only at June and December.)

### Strategic Professional (%)

| Session | SBL | SBR | AFM | APM | ATX | AAA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2026 | 52 | 50 | 44 | 40 | 50 | 42 |
| Dec 2025 | 50 | 48 | 45 | 41 | 50 | 38 |
| Sep 2025 | 51 | 48 | 44 | 40 | 53 | 40 |
| Jun 2025 | 51 | 49 | 46 | 40 | 49 | 40 |

### What the numbers actually say

1. **The difficulty cliff is at Applied Skills, not Strategic Professional.** BT sits at
   87–88% and LW at 81–82%; then PM drops to **40–45%**. That is a ~45-point fall inside one
   level transition. Students calibrate their expectations on BT/FA and are then blindsided by
   PM. **Learms should set expectations explicitly at the MA → PM boundary.**
2. **PM is harder than four of the six Strategic Professional papers.** PM at 40–45% is at or
   below SBL (50–52%), SBR (48–50%), AFM (44–46%) and ATX (49–53%). The label "Applied Skills"
   implies an easier tier than reality.
3. **APM and AAA are the persistent hardest**, 38–42% across all four sittings.
4. **Rates are remarkably stable session to session** (mostly ±3 points). This means a
   Learms difficulty model can safely use a fixed per-paper prior rather than tracking
   sessions — a useful simplification for Agent 1's BKT parameter seeding.
5. **Compounding matters and is never shown.** Passing 13 papers at an average ~55% first-time
   rate means the expected number of *sittings* far exceeds 13. At the Strategic Professional
   level alone, four papers at ~45% average implies an expected ~9 attempts. At £208–282 per
   attempt, that is the real cost driver — not the headline per-paper fee.

**ACCA is the only one of the three bodies publishing this openly.** ICMAP publishes no pass
rates at all (`../BLOCKERS-7.md` B-003). Any "which is harder" content that compares ACCA's
published 40% against ICMAP silence is comparing a number to an absence.

## 5. Practical Experience Requirement (PER)

36 months of relevant supervised experience, recorded against defined performance objectives
and signed off by a workplace mentor. PER may be completed before, during or after exams.

**Compared with ICAP's articleship, PER is structurally weaker as a student protection:**

| | ACCA PER | ICAP training |
|---|---|---|
| Employer must be approved | No (supervisor must be qualified/appropriate) | Yes — authorised Training Organisation |
| Minimum pay mandated | **No** | **Yes** — ICAP sets binding minimum stipends |
| Duration | 36 months | 30–42 months |
| Can be done anywhere | Yes — global | Pakistan (or approved overseas TO) |

**Finding:** PER's flexibility is genuinely ACCA's biggest advantage for people already
employed — and simultaneously its biggest weakness for school-leavers, because **there is no
stipend floor and no guaranteed placement**. An ACCA student in Pakistan with no job has no
route to PER, while an ICAP trainee's training *is* their job and is paid at a regulated
minimum. This asymmetry explains much of the "ACCA is for people who already have a job or
family support" pattern reported in community discussion.

## 7. Why ACCA still wins on mobility

180+ markets recognise ACCA; the qualification, the exams and the fees are identical
worldwide, and PER can be completed in any country with an appropriate supervisor. Neither
ICMAP's CMA nor ICAP's CA has an equivalent portability story — both are primarily recognised
in Pakistan and parts of the GCC, with reciprocal arrangements rather than direct recognition
elsewhere.

For a student whose goal is emigration, the GBP-denominated fee that looks like ACCA's biggest
weakness domestically is the price of the only one of the three credentials that travels.
This trade-off — **pay in hard currency now, gain optionality later** — is the honest framing,
and it is the framing Learms should use rather than a simple cost ranking.
