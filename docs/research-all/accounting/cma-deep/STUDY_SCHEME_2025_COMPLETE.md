## 2. Two designations, not one

A structural change that is widely missed in secondary write-ups: Study Scheme-2025 defines
**two exit designations** on one ladder.

| Designation | Awarded after | Indicative elapsed time from 12-year entry |
|---|---|---|
| **AFA — Affiliate Financial Accountant** | Operational Level + Managerial Level + Practical Training Modules Level-1 | 2.5 years |
| **CMA — Chartered Management Accountant** | AFA requirements + Strategic Level + Practical Training Modules Level-2 | 4 years |

Source: Circular §2, "Roadmap to Become Affiliate Financial Accountant (AFA) and Chartered
Management Accountant (CMA)".

The roadmap's own time markers are: 1-Year → Operational Level complete; 2-Year → Managerial
Level complete; 2.5-Year → AFA (PTM Level-1); 3.5-Year → Strategic Level complete; 4-Year →
CMA (PTM Level-2).

**Why this matters for Learms:** a student can be "an ICMAP student" at three very different
places on the ladder (pre-AFA, AFA, CMA-finalist). Any progress model that treats "CMA" as a
single binary goal will mis-model 100% of ICMAP users. Agent 1 should carry an explicit
`stage` attribute, not just a qualification name.

### Two findings worth Agent 2's attention

1. **Only two courses are genuinely new** (O2 and M4). Everything else is a renumbering,
   retitling, merge, or level-shift. Study material from the 2018/2023 scheme is therefore
   largely still content-valid for 16 of 18 courses — but the *codes* changed, so students
   searching for "S2 past papers" now get Audit & Assurance material when they need Advance
   Taxation. **This code collision is a concrete, solvable content problem for Learms.**
2. **Two topics were quietly removed from module titles**: Python (from PTM1) and ERP
   Solutions (from PTM4). Given that ERP skills are among the most demanded in the Pakistani
   market (see `../pakistan/JOB_MARKET_ANALYSIS.md`), losing the only ERP-titled module while
   employers ask for SAP/Oracle is a curriculum-to-market gap.

## 5. Entry routes — official, with the brief's error corrected

| Academic background | Entry point | Courses required | PTMs required | Stated duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **12-year education** | Operational Level-1 | **18 courses** ⚠️ | **6 PTMs** | 4 years |
| **14-year education** (commerce/business) | Managerial Level-1 | **12 courses** | **3 PTMs** | 2.5 years |
| **16-year education** (commerce/business) | Managerial Level-2 | **09 courses** | **3 PTMs** | 2 years |
| 14- or 16-year **non-commerce/non-business** | Managerial Level-1 | 12 courses | 3 PTMs | 2.5 years, **plus 3 weeks' free Basic Accounting Course (BAC)** |

Source: Circular §2 ("Entry Routes" block) and §4.

⚠️ **The brief stated "12-Year Education: 09 Courses + 6 PTM". That is wrong.** The official
circular states **"12-Year Education: 4-Year 18 Courses + 6 Mandatory Practical Training
Modules."** The "09 courses" figure belongs to the **16-year** route. This is the single most
consequential factual error in the brief: it understates the workload of the most common
school-leaver entry route by exactly half.

### Waivers for 12-year entrants

| Prior qualification | Courses eligible for waiver |
|---|---|
| Intermediate (Commerce) | O1 Fundamentals of Financial Accounting |
| A-Levels (Cambridge) | O1 Fundamentals of Financial Accounting; O4 Business Economics; O5 Business Mathematics and Statistics |

Waivers are **not free**: "Any eligible student may obtain waiver in above courses on payment
of **50% of prescribed tuition fee** for respective course in addition to prescribed fee for
registration." (Circular §4.3)

**A-Levels students get 3 waivers; Inter-Commerce students get 1.** For a Pakistani
middle-class family this is a regressive structure: the more expensive prior schooling
(A-Levels) unlocks more fee relief.

### Certificate policy (§4.6)

Students entering directly at Managerial Level-1 or -2 on 14/16-year education get **no
waiver or level-completion certificate**. Only students taking partial waivers on the basis of
12-year education or a professional certification receive a waiver certificate. Practically:
a graduate entrant who drops out at Managerial Level has **no ICMAP paper** to show for it.

## 6. Practical Training Modules — the rule as written, and its ambiguity

Circular §5, verbatim:

> "All six practical training modules are mandatory for the students having 12-year education,
> whereas; all three modules practical training level-1 for those students having 14 or
> 16-year education."

> "Moreover, all practical training modules are not mandatory for members/affiliates of
> professional accounting bodies approved by ICMA International; however, partially qualified
> students of professional accounting bodies who have academic qualification Intermediate/
> A-Level are required to take all practical training modules."

**Ambiguity flagged:** §5 says 14/16-year entrants take PTM **Level-1** only (PTM1–PTM3), yet
the §2 roadmap places PTM Level-2 (PTM4–PTM6) after the Strategic Level as a CMA requirement,
and the §9 progression rules describe eligibility for PTM Level-2 for anyone who has passed
all courses up to Strategic Level-1. Read literally, a 16-year entrant would obtain CMA
without ever taking Audit Procedures, Practical Aspects of Banking & Finance, or Strategic
Management Analysis. Either §5 means "three modules at Level-1 *plus* Level-2 as per the
roadmap", or graduates genuinely skip PTM Level-2.

**Not resolved from public documents.** Logged in `BLOCKERS-7.md`. Learms must not state a
PTM requirement for graduate entrants as fact until ICMAP confirms.

### PTM examination eligibility gates (§9)

- **PTM Level-1:** passed all courses up to Managerial Level-1 **and** appeared in Managerial Level-2.
- **PTM Level-2:** passed all courses up to Strategic Level-1 **and** appearing in all Strategic Level-2 papers.

Note the asymmetry: eligibility requires having *appeared* (not passed) in the level above.
This lets students run PTMs in parallel with their hardest exam sittings — a scheduling
pressure point Learms can help plan around.

## 7. Lecture and credit hours — and an inconsistency in the official document

Circular §7, verbatim:

> "The Lecture hours for each course are 90 and the credit hours are 6, totalling 1260 lecture
> hours and 144 credit hours for the complete study scheme."

**The arithmetic does not reconcile:**

| Reading | Modules counted | Lecture hours implied | Credit hours implied |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 courses only | 18 | 18 × 90 = **1,620** | 18 × 6 = 108 |
| 18 courses + 6 PTM | 24 | 24 × 90 = **2,160** | 24 × 6 = **144** ✅ |
| Whatever yields 1,260 | 14 | **1,260** ✅ | 14 × 6 = 84 |

The **144 credit hours** figure is consistent with **24 modules × 6 credits** (18 courses +
6 PTMs). The **1,260 lecture hours** figure is consistent with neither 18 nor 24 modules at
90 hours each. The most likely explanation is that PTMs carry fewer lecture hours than
courses and the 90-hour figure applies to courses only, but 18 × 90 = 1,620 ≠ 1,260 either.

**Treat 1,260 as unreliable.** Use **144 credit hours / 24 modules** as the planning anchor.
Logged in `BLOCKERS-7.md` and `../CONTRADICTIONS.md`.

**Study-time estimate for Learms (modelled, Tier E):** at 6 months per level and 3 courses per
level, a full-time-equivalent 90 lecture hours per course means ~270 contact hours per
6-month level, i.e. **~10.4 contact hours/week per level**, before any self-study. Standard
professional-exam guidance puts self-study at 2–3× contact time, implying **30–40 hours/week
total** for a student carrying a full level. Most ICMAP students work full-time. This is the
arithmetic root of the "no time" complaint in `STUDENT_PROBLEMS.md`.

## 8. Examination eligibility (§8)

| Route | Requirements to be allowed to sit |
|---|---|
| **Coaching classes** | ≥ **60% lecture attendance**; appeared in the **mandatory presentation** for the course; all outstanding dues cleared; prescribed exam fee paid |
| **Distance Learning Program (DLP)** | All outstanding dues cleared; appeared in the **mandatory presentation** for the course; prescribed exam fee paid |

Two things Learms should note:
1. **DLP has no attendance requirement** — it trades supervision for flexibility. This is why
   working students concentrate in DLP, and why DLP material quality (see
   `STUDENT_PROBLEMS.md`) has outsized impact.
2. **A mandatory presentation applies to both routes.** Presentation skills are therefore a
   hard gate on exam entry, not a soft extra. Learms content should include presentation prep
   per course — this is an under-served, clearly-mandated need.

## 9. Examination progression scheme (§9)

Core rule: **maximum six courses per sitting, sequential, no skipping a lower level**, and the
student must have enrolled **at least one month before examination forms are submitted**.

| Current position | May sit up to 6 courses reaching as far as |
|---|---|
| Joined on 12-year education, or existing student | Operational Level-2 |
| Waivers in some OL-1/OL-2 courses | remaining courses up to Operational Level-2 |
| Passed/waived all or some OL-1 or OL-2 courses | Managerial Level-1 |
| Joined at Managerial Level-1 (14-year, or 16-year non-commerce) | Managerial Level-2 |
| Passed/waived all OL-1 and some OL-2 or ML-1 | Managerial Level-2 |
| Joined at Managerial Level-2 (16-year commerce/business) | Strategic Level-1 |
| Passed/waived all OL-1 & OL-2 and some ML-1 or ML-2 | Strategic Level-1 |
| Passed/waived all up to ML-1, and some ML-2 or SL-1 | Strategic Level-2 |

The Examination Committee may consider case-by-case requests outside these parameters,
"provided there shall be no clash of examination papers for a candidate in the same
examination session."

**Modelling implication for Agent 1:** the progression scheme is a *reachability constraint
over a level DAG*, layered on top of the concept-level prerequisite DAG. These are two
distinct graphs and should not be conflated. An ICMAP planner needs: (a) concept prerequisites
for *learning order*, (b) level progression rules for *legal sitting order*. A schedule that
is pedagogically ideal can still be against the rules.

## 10. Transitional arrangements for Study Scheme-2018 students (§6)

- All students who had not completed CMA under the existing scheme were **merged into Study
  Scheme-2025 with effect from 15 July 2025** (note: §6 says 15 July, §1 says the scheme is
  effective 16 July — a one-day internal inconsistency in the circular).
- Students who had completed examination eligibility or already attempted a course under the
  2018/2023 scheme may continue **at the regular exam fee for up to six (6) attempts**. After
  six attempts they pay **double the examination fee** then applicable.
- Existing students **do not have to re-enrol in coaching/DLP** for equivalent remaining
  courses if they completed those requirements under the old scheme. Voluntary re-joining
  costs **50% of the prescribed coaching fee**.
- Re-coaching generally (per the fee page): students who already completed eligibility in a
  subject may re-admit "for updating their studies" at **50% of the prescribed fee**,
  non-refundable.

**The six-attempt cliff is a real financial trap** and is the closest thing ICMAP has to an
attempt limit. See `EXAM_PATTERN.md` §"Attempt limits".

### Findings

1. **The advertised "cheap" entry route is the most expensive qualification.** A 12-year
   entrant pays ~PKR 727K; a 16-year entrant pays ~PKR 464K for the same designation — a
   **PKR 263K penalty (57% more) for entering straight from Intermediate.** The high
   registration fee for graduates (63,000 vs 12,500) is nowhere near enough to offset the nine
   extra courses they skip.
2. Under a *pure cost* lens, an Intermediate student is financially better off completing a
   B.Com/BBA first and entering at Managerial Level — **but** that adds 2–4 years of
   university time and its own tuition, so it is only rational if the degree is being taken
   anyway. Learms should present this as an explicit, quantified decision, not folklore.
3. **These totals are floors, not expectations.** They exclude resits (see the six-attempt
   double-fee cliff), re-coaching at 50%, waiver purchases at 50% of tuition, books, travel to
   exam centres in 9 cities, and the 10% instalment surcharge.

## 13. The "PKR 93,000 per module" claim — resolved

The brief asserts "High Fees: Modules costing approximately PKR 93,000 each".

**Verdict: the number is real but the unit is wrong in most retellings.**

| Interpretation | Computed value | Match? |
|---|---|---|
| Per **course/paper** (1 subject) | 23,000 – 39,500 | ❌ Off by ~3× |
| Per **level** (3 courses, 6 months) at 2025 rates | 69,000 – 118,500 | ✅ ML-1 = 96,000, ML-2 = 99,000 |
| Per **level** at **2024** rates (ML-2) | 3 × (23,700 + 6,400) = **90,300** | ✅ Closest match to "≈93,000" |
| Per **level** at **2024** rates (ML-1) | 3 × (22,500 + 6,400) = 86,700 | ✅ Same band |

**Conclusion:** "≈PKR 93,000 per module" corresponds to **one six-month LEVEL of three
subjects at Managerial stage, priced at 2024–25 rates**. It is *not* the price of a single
paper. Anyone quoting it as a per-paper figure inflates ICMAP's cost ~3×; anyone dismissing it
as false understates a genuine grievance. At current (2026) rates the equivalent figure is
**PKR 99,900–102,900 per managerial level** and **PKR 117,000–123,000 per strategic level**.

This resolution matters: the grievance is legitimate and is *worsening*, and Learms loses
credibility if it repeats the figure with the wrong unit.
