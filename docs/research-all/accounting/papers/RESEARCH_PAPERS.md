## 1. The state of the field, honestly

The peer-reviewed literature specifically on **Pakistani** accounting education is **small** —
a handful of papers, dominated by one research partnership. The literature on accounting
education *in general* (AI integration, gamification, competency frameworks) is large but almost
entirely set in the US, UK, South Africa, Malaysia and Indonesia.

**There is no published academic study of ICMAP students specifically.** Not one was found.
Everything in this research about ICMAP student experience is either (a) inferred from university
student studies, (b) derived from ICMAP's own documents, or (c) Tier-D grievance material. This
is the single largest evidential gap in the whole project and it is stated here rather than
concealed by extrapolation.

## 2. Tier A — peer-reviewed, Pakistan-specific, primary evidence

### 2.1 Awais & Bigoni (2026) — accounting education and neocolonialism ★ NEW

> Awais, W. and Bigoni, M. (2026) 'Accounting education and neocolonialism in Pakistan'.
> Author accepted manuscript: https://kar.kent.ac.uk/112699/1/Accounting%20education%20Pakistan%20final.pdf
> (Kent Academic Repository, University of Kent, accessed 2026-09-30)

**Method:** **28 interviews** with accounting **policymakers, educators and students**, plus
document analysis of **degree handbooks and module guides** at **five highly regarded Pakistani
universities**.

**Why it is distinctive:** the authors state it is **the first study to bring together all three
actor groups** who shape accounting education. Prior work — including their own 2025 paper —
looked at one group at a time.

**Central findings:**
- Accounting curricula in Pakistan "usually focus on the needs of private corporations" rather
  than on students' capacity to contribute to their socio-political and economic environment.
- **Professional bodies exert control over university curricula** through accreditation and
  exemptions, and "may even prevent educators and universities from acting in the interest of
  society at large".
- A direct, attributable claim about institutional capture: *"The senior management of these
  professional bodies, notably ICAP, consists of individuals most of whom are either employed or
  have ties with large Western corporations, especially the 'Big Four'."*
- Accounting education operates as "an indirect mechanism to maintain dominant structures in the
  existing global order" — students are taught to treat Western accounting systems as the
  reference point.

**The authors' own proposed remedy** — and it is unusually concrete for a critical-accounting
paper: the process "will be long and difficult; however, initial **'non-traumatic' but effective
measures** can be introduced in a relatively short period of time", specifically exposing students
to a broader definition of accounting, and **"more focus on Pakistan in the teaching of
accounting"**.

**Direct relevance to Learms:** this is the second independent peer-reviewed source, from
different informants and a different method, arriving at the **same prescription** as the 2025
paper: *localise the content*. Two papers, three actor groups, 48 interviews combined, one
recommendation. For a platform deciding where to spend authoring effort, that is about as strong
a signal as this literature can produce.

**Caveat:** an author accepted manuscript in an institutional repository. Journal, volume and DOI
were **not captured**; the exemplar metadata beyond year and title is unverified. Cite as AAM.

### 2.2 Bigoni & Awais (2025) — student resistance ★ ANALYSED SEPARATELY

> Bigoni, M. and Awais, W. (2025) '"I am no longer part of this rat race": Student resistance to
> neoliberal accounting education in Pakistan', *Accounting Forum*, 49(2), pp. 472–497.
> DOI: 10.1080/01559982.2024.2309595
> Open access VoR: https://shura.shu.ac.uk/33485/3/Awais-IAmNoLongerPart(VoR).pdf

**≥20 interviews, five Pakistani universities, snowball sampling. Theory: James C. Scott's
infrapolitics.** Students describe themselves as "robots" and "beasts of burden"; reject the
recycled Enron canon; ask for content about "local jeweller shops or restaurants".

**Full analysis: `BIGONI_AWAIS_2024_RAT_RACE.md`.** It is the most important single source in
this research and is treated at length there rather than summarised twice.

## 3. Tier B — Pakistan-specific, peer-reviewed, but dated

### 3.1 Perception of University Graduates about Accounting Education in Pakistan

> 'Perception of University Graduates about Accounting Education in Pakistan'.
> ResearchGate publication 338885916 —
> https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338885916_Perception_of_University_Graduates_about_Accounting_Education_in_Pakistan
> (accessed 2026-09-30)

**Method:** thematic analysis of **semi-structured interviews with 50 accounting professionals**
(university graduates) across sectors of Pakistan's labour market.

**Three problems identified:**
1. **Deficiency of skills**
2. **Gap between theory and practice**
3. **Non-completion of accounting courses** — *"Most of them (78%) responded that only half of
   their courses would hardly be completed during their [studies]."*

**Remedies proposed by respondents:** curriculum revision (36% of respondents), internships,
introduction of accounting software, teacher training workshops, and **HEC enforcement of
standardisation and harmonisation** across universities.

**The 78% finding is the most operationally useful statistic located in this entire literature
review**, and it is badly under-cited. If most graduates report that only half the syllabus was
actually taught, then:
- Self-study platforms are not a supplement in Pakistan — they are covering a **structural
  delivery failure**.
- Coverage-tracking against the *official* syllabus (not against what a teacher got through) is a
  genuinely differentiated feature.
- It independently corroborates ICMAP's mandatory-coaching-with-60%-attendance model as a
  response to a real problem, even while that model is criticised on cost grounds elsewhere in
  this research.

**⚠️ Dating caveat — important.** The ResearchGate record carries a **2018** page date. The
research constraint for this project is 2025–2026 data. **This paper is therefore used for
*structural* insight (problems that are plausibly persistent) and NOT for any current-state
claim.** The 78% figure must be presented with its date, never as a 2026 statistic. Journal,
volume, issue and DOI were **not captured** — only the ResearchGate record was seen.

**Corroboration note:** its "theory–practice gap" finding is echoed independently by the two
Bigoni/Awais papers, which raises confidence that the *problem* persists even though the
*measurement* is old.

### The gamification finding, read against Bigoni & Awais
The systematic review reports gamification improving motivation and engagement, framed via
Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness).

**But Bigoni & Awais show Pakistani students disengaging because they judge the content
irrelevant** — a *relatedness and meaning* failure, not a *stimulation* failure. Gamifying
content that students consider foreign is likely to read as one more thing being done *to* them
by an institution, to a population that already describes itself as "robots".

**Recommendation:** treat the gamification literature as unvalidated in this context. Localise
content first; measure; only then layer engagement mechanics. Recorded in `../RECOMMENDATIONS.md`.

## 5. Cross-cutting observation: who studies Pakistani accounting students?

Two researchers — **Waqas Awais** and **Michele Bigoni** — account for essentially the entire
recent primary-evidence base, across two papers and 48 interviews, based at **Sheffield Hallam**
and the **University of Kent**.

This is worth stating plainly for three reasons:
1. **Concentration risk.** A near-complete dependence on one research partnership means one
   theoretical lens (critical/postcolonial accounting). Their findings are well-evidenced, but
   they are not triangulated by researchers working from a different tradition.
2. **Both are UK-based.** The critique of Pakistani accounting education as Western-framed is
   itself produced from within Western institutions. The authors are alert to this; readers of
   this research should be too.
3. **It is an opportunity.** Learms would sit on exactly the dataset — anonymised, consented —
   that this field lacks: large-N behavioural evidence about how Pakistani accounting students
   actually study and where they actually fail. That is a genuine research-contribution path,
   and it makes Agent 3's privacy and consent design a precondition rather than an overhead.
