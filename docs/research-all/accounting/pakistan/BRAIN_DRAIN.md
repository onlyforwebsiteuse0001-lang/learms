# Emigration of Pakistani Accounting Professionals

**Area:** 10 (Pakistan industry & job market)
**Researched:** 2026-09-30
**Health warning:** this is the topic where unsourced statistics circulate most freely in
Pakistani careers content. This file separates what is evidenced from what is merely repeated.

---

## 1. The brief's claim, and its status

> **Brief claim:** "87% of ACCA students want to go abroad; 69% want to start their own business."

**Status: origin not located.** No ACCA survey, journal article, press release or news report
containing this paired statistic was found. ACCA does publish global talent and mobility research
containing comparable questions, and Pakistani emigration is independently documented — but the
**specific 87%/69% pairing was not traced to any source.**

**It is therefore reported here as a claim in circulation, not as a finding.** Logged as
`../BLOCKERS-7.md` B-009.

**Instruction to downstream agents:** do not reproduce these figures in Learms. If a
migration-intent framing is needed, use the structural evidence in §2, which requires no
contested statistic.

## 2. What can be said without a contested statistic

The structural case for emigration pressure rests on facts established elsewhere in this research,
each individually sourced to a Tier-A body. No survey is required.

### 2.1 One of the three qualifications is portable and two are not
- **ACCA** is recognised across ~180 markets and priced identically worldwide — the Pakistan fee
  page publishes the **same GBP fees as global**, confirmed directly.
- **CA (ICAP)** and **CMA (ICMAP)** are recognised principally in Pakistan and the GCC.

A student who selects ACCA has, by construction, selected the portable option. The qualification
choice *is* the migration signal — which makes survey data about intent somewhat redundant.

### 2.2 ACCA students already pay in hard currency
ACCA fees are GBP-denominated: registration £89, annual subscription £140 (2026), Applied Skills
£160 standard. A Pakistani ACCA student is already absorbing **unhedged rupee-depreciation risk
over 3+ years**. Students paying in GBP to earn a globally-portable credential are a population
whose revealed preference needs no questionnaire.

### 2.3 The domestic salary ceiling is low relative to the cost of the credential
The only Tier-A salary anchor in Pakistani accounting is ICAP's **regulated stipend floor**, which
reaches **PKR 93,500/month** for a qualified trainee in their final six months. Defensible
post-qualification ranges are PKR 110,000–250,000/month for a newly-qualified CA and
PKR 120,000–200,000 for an ACCA member (see `../career/SALARY_DATA_2025_2026.md`).

Against a GCC or UK salary for the same credential, the gap is large. That is the mechanism. It
does not require an 87% statistic to be true.

### 2.4 ICMAP itself operates outbound pathways as a product line
ICMAP's own published fee structure prices conversion routes to foreign bodies:

| Pathway | Published ICMAP fee (PKR) |
|---|---|
| ACCA–FCCA MPP | 13,200 + 73,700 + 20,400 |
| CIMA LLG — ACMA | 63,500 |
| CIMA LLG — FCMA | 53,200 |
| CPA Canada | 32,600 + 11,800 + 120,200 |

**This is the strongest available evidence on the question, and it comes from ICMAP itself.** A
national professional body does not build and price four outbound conversion routes — including
a **PKR 164,600 CPA Canada route** — unless there is sustained demand from its own members to
qualify somewhere else. The institute has monetised the exit.

*Source: https://www.icmap.com.pk/fee_structure.aspx (Tier A, accessed 2026-09-30).*

### 2.5 The academic literature frames it as a systemic outcome, not individual ambition
Awais & Bigoni (2026) argue Pakistani accounting education teaches students to treat **Western
accounting systems as the reference point**, functioning as "an indirect mechanism to maintain
dominant structures in the existing global order". On that reading, emigration is not a leak from
the system — it is the system working as designed. Bigoni & Awais (2025) document the terminal
form: *"I am no longer part of this rat race."*

Note that in the 2025 paper, exit means leaving **accounting**, not necessarily leaving Pakistan.
**Two different exits are routinely conflated in Pakistani careers writing** — emigration and
occupational abandonment. They have different causes and different remedies, and a platform that
wants to reduce attrition needs to know which one it is looking at.

## 3. Implications for Learms

| # | Implication | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ask users their intent — don't infer it.** "Do you intend to work abroad?" changes the correct recommendation more than any other single question. ACCA for mobility; CA for domestic practice and stipend funding. | §2.1 |
| 2 | **Show currency risk explicitly for ACCA.** A 3-year GBP commitment is a financial product decision. No competitor page reviewed discloses it. | §2.2 |
| 3 | **Surface ICMAP's own conversion pathways.** They are published, priced, and almost never presented to members as options. | §2.4 |
| 4 | **Distinguish "leaving Pakistan" from "leaving accounting" in retention analytics.** Conflating them will produce meaningless churn metrics. | §2.5 |
| 5 | **Do not build features on the 87% figure.** | §1 |

## 4. What was not researched — stated honestly

- **No Pakistan Bureau of Statistics or Bureau of Emigration data** was retrieved on
  professional-category outflows.
- **No ACCA Pakistan member/student counts** were retrieved, so no emigration *rate* can be
  calculated for any body.
- **No GCC or UK salary data** was gathered, so the domestic/overseas gap is asserted
  directionally and not quantified.
- **No data on return migration.**

These are real gaps. The conclusions above are deliberately limited to what the retrieved
Tier-A material supports.
