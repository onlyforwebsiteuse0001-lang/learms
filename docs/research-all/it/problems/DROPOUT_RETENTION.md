## Topic: CS attrition is a known long-running concern
### Source: Beaubouef and Mason, 2005
### Key Finding: The paper investigates causes of high attrition rates for CS students and frames attrition as a serious problem for universities and the supply of technologically competent professionals.
### Relevance to Learms: Learms should track early-warning signals and provide support before students silently quit.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1145/1083431.1083474

## Topic: Programming self-efficacy affects retention risk
### Source: Chen et al., 2024; Rosenstein et al., 2020
### Key Finding: Recent CS impostor phenomenon research reports links between impostor feelings and code comprehension outcomes; Rosenstein et al. specifically studied prevalence among CS students.
### Relevance to Learms: Learms should normalize struggle, show progress evidence, and avoid ranking UI that increases shame.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-61569-6_2 ; https://doi.org/10.1145/3328778.3366815

## Topic: Failure to independently solve programming problems can feed dropout
### Source: McCracken et al., 2001; Luxton-Reilly et al., 2018
### Key Finding: Introductory programming research shows persistent difficulty in students developing programming competence.
### Relevance to Learms: Learms should combine small wins with real independent project tasks to build confidence and competence.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1145/572133.572137 ; https://doi.org/10.1145/3293881.3295779
