## Topic: CS2023 increases visibility of mathematical and statistical foundations
### Source: ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI Joint Task Force, 2023
### Key Finding: CS2023 emphasizes mathematical and statistical foundations because areas such as AI, machine learning, robotics, data science, and security require stronger quantitative preparation.
### Relevance to Learms: Learms should show math as contextual prerequisite modules tied to goals, not as a generic barrier.
### Citation: https://csed.acm.org/

## Topic: Data science requires statistics, computing, governance, and communication
### Source: ACM Data Science Task Force, 2021
### Key Finding: ACM data science competencies include statistics/mathematics, programming, data acquisition/management/governance, machine learning, visualization, security/privacy, and professionalism.
### Relevance to Learms: Data learners need statistics and communication paths alongside Python notebooks.
### Citation: https://dstf.acm.org/

## Topic: Embedded/computer engineering requires math plus hardware reasoning
### Source: ACM/IEEE-CS, 2016
### Key Finding: CE2016 positions computer engineering as integrating electrical engineering, software engineering, computer science, and mathematics, including embedded systems, architecture, networks, and signal processing.
### Relevance to Learms: Embedded/IoT/robotics paths need math modules for signals, timing, control, and hardware interfaces.
### Citation: https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/education/ce2016-final-report.pdf

## Learms Recommendations

- Use math diagnostic micro-assessments per career goal.
- Label math modules by practical payoff: “Linear algebra for embeddings,” “Probability for model metrics,” “Discrete math for graphs.”
- Avoid blocking web/mobile beginners with unnecessary calculus.
- Require stronger math gates for ML/DL/robotics/graphics/crypto.
