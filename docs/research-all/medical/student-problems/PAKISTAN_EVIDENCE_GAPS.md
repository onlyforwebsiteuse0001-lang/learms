# Pakistan medical-student problem evidence gaps

## Topic
National representativeness

### Source
Pakistan systematic reviews and multisite/single-site primary studies. [MED-092–MED-100, MED-102]

### Key Finding
The evidence base is dominated by cross-sectional self-report studies, often from urban institutions. The 2026 depression review found extreme heterogeneity, and the reviewed burnout, sleep, learning-environment, financial and mistreatment studies use different samples and instruments. A current representative national longitudinal cohort was **not found**.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not publish a national “wellbeing score.” Future research should stratify transparently by province, public/private sector, program, year, residence, disability and other ethically justified characteristics while protecting small groups.

### Citation
Ebrahimi et al. (2026), doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2026-116544. Azim et al. (2022), doi:10.47391/JPMA.4042. Source details [MED-094–MED-100, MED-102] in `SOURCE_REGISTER.csv`.

## Topic
Longitudinal outcomes and causal pathways

### Source
Area 4 evidence-design audit. [MED-094, MED-096–MED-102]

### Key Finding
Longitudinal Pakistan evidence linking training exposures to subsequent mental-health, dropout, academic, patient-safety or career outcomes was **not found** in the reviewed set. Cross-sectional regressions do not establish direction.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not claim that improving app use will reduce depression, burnout, failure or attrition. Area 5 must evaluate interventions independently.

### Citation
See design and limitations in `student-problems/PROBLEM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.csv`.

## Topic
Service capacity and utilization

### Source
PM&DC support standards and recent qualitative evidence. [MED-001, MED-095, MED-106]

### Key Finding
Standards require support access, while a small qualitative study reports perceived absence, poor visibility, access barriers and confidentiality concerns. No current national dataset was found for counselling staffing, hours, utilization, waiting time, continuity, cost, language, accessibility or outcomes.

### Relevance to Learms
A service directory needs first-party verification and expiry dates. Policy-required is not the same as operationally available.

### Citation
PM&DC (2024), undergraduate guidance and medical-college accreditation standard. Khurshid et al. (2025), doi:10.1186/s12909-025-07762-y.

## Topic
Suicide and acute-risk data

### Source
Global review and WHO guidance; Pakistan systematic depression review. [MED-087, MED-092, MED-104]

### Key Finding
Global suicidal-ideation evidence exists, but a current representative Pakistan medical-student dataset on ideation, attempts, deaths, service use and follow-up was **not found** in this pass. A verified, universally applicable Pakistan crisis-contact dataset was also not found.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not localize a global prevalence as Pakistan's and do not launch crisis routing with unverified contacts.

### Citation
Rotenstein et al. (2016), doi:10.1001/jama.2016.17324. Ebrahimi et al. (2026), doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2026-116544. WHO (2023), digital interventions evidence-centre page.

## Topic
Harassment reporting outcomes

### Source
Older Pakistan studies and current HEC policy. [MED-097, MED-098, MED-105]

### Key Finding
Current national evidence on incident prevalence, reporting rates, inquiry timelines, retaliation, outcomes, appeals and user trust was **not found**. Policy describes intended procedure, not measured implementation.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not display a “safe college” badge based on policy presence. A verified route directory should disclose evidence date and source.

### Citation
Ahmer et al. (2008), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0003889. Shoukat et al. (2010), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0013429. HEC Pakistan revised harassment policy.

## Topic
Missing and under-described groups

### Source
Area 4 evidence audit. [MED-092–MED-102]

### Key Finding
Comparable current evidence was not found for disabled learners, gender-diverse learners, religious/ethnic minorities, rural learners, students studying away from family, international students in Pakistan, BDS-only cohorts, students who leave training, and students in smaller cities. Some studies report sex or residence but do not support a comprehensive equity analysis.

### Relevance to Learms
Treat missing evidence as unknown, not as absence of need. Co-design and accessibility work should not wait for a prevalence estimate.

### Citation
Registered Area 4 sources [MED-092–MED-102]; see source-specific population and limitation fields in `PROBLEM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.csv`.
