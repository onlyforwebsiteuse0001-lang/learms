# Financial pressure and career uncertainty

## Topic
Pakistan student narratives show multiple financial pathways

### Source
Asim et al. qualitative study of Pakistani medical students. [MED-102]

### Key Finding
Five themes covered financial stressors in medical education, academic effects, career-choice effects, health/wellbeing practices, and desired policy/intervention responses. Qualitative themes establish mechanisms and lived experience, not population prevalence or the cost faced by an average student.

### Relevance to Learms
Financial support needs should include fee/scholarship navigation, hidden-cost planning, and career information. Never estimate a learner's wealth, debt, or ability to pay from institution type or location.

### Citation
Asim N et al. (2025). Exploring the perceptions of medical students about the effects of financial stresses in Pakistan. *Pakistan Journal of Medical Sciences*, 41(2), 455–460. doi:10.12669/pjms.41.2.9286.

## Topic
Financial pressure intersects with family expectations and uncertainty

### Source
Khurshid, Khurshid & Toor qualitative focus group with 12 purposively selected Rawalpindi/Islamabad medical students. [MED-095]

### Key Finding
Participants described family financial sacrifice, pressure to justify that sacrifice through achievement, and concerns about postgraduate pathways, unemployment, underemployment and compensation. The selected sample supplies context but cannot estimate how frequently these concerns occur nationally.

### Relevance to Learms
Career maps should clearly separate exam eligibility, selection, training, credential and registration, as Area 3 does. Avoid salary promises or “guaranteed pathway” language that may intensify financial decisions.

### Citation
Khurshid S, Khurshid S, Toor HK. (2025). Burnout as a mental health challenge among medical students in Pakistan: a qualitative study of its triggers, impacts, and support needs. *BMC Medical Education*, 25, 1190. doi:10.1186/s12909-025-07762-y.

## Topic
Low household income was associated with burnout subtype scores in one study

### Source
Irshad et al. cross-sectional study of 284 students at Shifa College of Medicine using BCSQ-12. [MED-096]

### Key Finding
Low family household income was significantly associated with burnout and with the neglect subtype score in the reported analysis; sleep deprivation was also associated. The design cannot establish that income caused burnout, and one Islamabad institution cannot represent Pakistan.

### Relevance to Learms
Offer financial-support discovery independently of a burnout screen. Do not use income as a hidden wellbeing-risk feature.

### Citation
Irshad K, Ashraf I, Azam F, Shaheen A. (2022). Burnout prevalence and associated factors in medical students in integrated modular curriculum: A cross-sectional study. *Pakistan Journal of Medical Sciences*, 38(4), 801–806. doi:10.12669/pjms.38.4.5052.

## Topic
PM&DC expects financial support and career guidance

### Source
PM&DC Guidelines for Undergraduate Medical Education (2024). [MED-001]

### Key Finding
The guidance says institutions should provide accessible and confidential academic, social, psychological and financial support services and career guidance. The document supports a service expectation, but this review did not verify implementation quality at each college.

### Relevance to Learms
Institution profiles can include `financial_support_available`, `career_counselling_available`, source URL, evidence date and institution-confirmed contact. Unknown must remain “not verified,” not “no.”

### Citation
Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (2024). *Guidelines for Undergraduate Medical Education MBBS Curriculum*, section on student counselling and support. Official PM&DC PDF, accessed 2026-09-30.

## Topic
No national amount or prevalence was verified

### Source
Area 2 fee research and Area 4 reviewed studies. [MED-001, MED-057, MED-102]

### Key Finding
Private tuition caps and public fees do not capture transport, hostel, books, devices, examination, application, clinical-placement or lost-income costs. The reviewed qualitative evidence does not yield a representative financial-stress prevalence or national total cost of medical education.

### Relevance to Learms
A budgeting tool must use user-entered amounts and dated official fees, present ranges, and label omitted cost categories. It must not infer affordability or recommend debt.

### Citation
PM&DC (2025–2026) private-college tuition-cap notices [MED-057]; PM&DC (2024) undergraduate guidance [MED-001]; Asim et al. (2025), doi:10.12669/pjms.41.2.9286 [MED-102].
