# Sleep, fatigue and digital distraction

## Topic
Sleep problems are common in global medical-student samples

### Source
Jahrami et al. systematic review/meta-analysis of 43 studies and 18,619 students from 13 countries. [MED-090]

### Key Finding
Mean pooled sleep duration was 6.3 hours/night (95% CI 6.0–6.6); pooled poor sleep quality by PSQI was 55% (95% CI 48–62); excessive daytime sleepiness was 31.0% (95% CI 24.4–37.7). Country differences and observational methods limit transfer to a specific learner or college.

### Relevance to Learms
Study planning should protect sleep by default: flexible reminders, quiet hours and no reward for late-night activity. These are humane defaults, not treatment.

### Citation
Jahrami H, Dewald-Kaufmann J, Faris MA-I, et al. (2020). Prevalence of sleep problems among medical students: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Public Health*, 28, 605–622. doi:10.1007/s10389-019-01064-6.

## Topic
Pakistan academic stress and sleep are associated

### Source
Waqas et al. cross-sectional study of 263 students at one private Lahore medical school using PSS-14 and PSQI. [MED-099]

### Key Finding
59.7% were in the study's high-stress category and 77.0% were poor sleepers by PSQI; 27.8% reported under five hours sleep. Stress score predicted poorer sleep score in the reported logistic model. The authors state that cross-sectional design prevents causal or temporal inference.

### Relevance to Learms
Exam-load and sleep check-ins may be offered separately. Learms must not tell a student that stress caused poor sleep, or that sleep alone explains grades.

### Citation
Waqas A, Khan S, Sharif W, Khalid U, Ali A. (2015). Association of academic stress with sleeping difficulties in medical students of a Pakistani medical school: a cross sectional survey. *PeerJ*, 3, e840. doi:10.7717/peerj.840.

## Topic
Smartphone overuse and poor sleep correlate, but labels require care

### Source
Leow et al. systematic review/meta-analysis of 16 studies and 9,466 medical students. [MED-101]

### Key Finding
The meta-analysis estimated “smartphone addiction” at 39% (95% CI 30–50), poor sleep at 57% (95% CI 48–66), average daily use at 4.90 hours, and a low positive correlation between smartphone-addiction and PSQI scores (r=0.30). Heterogeneity was high and the evidence was observational. The review notes that use for medical education remained unclear.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not equate total device time with harmful use: educational, social and support uses differ. Prefer user-controlled focus modes and notification batching over shame labels or forced lockouts.

### Citation
Leow MQH, Chiang J, Chua TJX, Wang S, Tan NC. (2023). The relationship between smartphone addiction and sleep among medical students: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *PLOS ONE*, 18(9), e0290724. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0290724.

## Topic
Fatigue can be academic, clinical, emotional, or sleep-related

### Source
Pakistan qualitative burnout study and PM&DC support/accreditation standards. [MED-095, MED-106]

### Key Finding
Participants in the small qualitative study described long clinical days, study demands, limited recovery and emotional strain as interconnected. PM&DC's 2024 medical-college standard requires counselling access and wellbeing mechanisms. Neither source establishes a single fatigue threshold or a safe maximum study duration.

### Relevance to Learms
Represent fatigue as a self-described state, not an inferred trait. Break prompts should be dismissible and should never penalize a learner's streak or progress.

### Citation
Khurshid S, Khurshid S, Toor HK. (2025), *BMC Medical Education* 25:1190, doi:10.1186/s12909-025-07762-y. Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (2024), *Accreditation Standards for Medical College up to 100 MBBS Students*.

## Not found

A representative national longitudinal dataset connecting Pakistani medical students' device use, objectively measured sleep, clinical schedules and academic outcomes was **not found**. No reviewed source supports a universal daily screen-time limit for Learms.
