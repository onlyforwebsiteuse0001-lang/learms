# Help-seeking and safety boundaries

## Topic
A treatment gap was visible in the global depression review

### Source
Rotenstein et al. systematic review/meta-analysis. [MED-087]

### Key Finding
Among students who screened positive for depression, 15.7% reportedly sought psychiatric treatment. The underlying studies varied, so this is not a Pakistan utilization estimate, but it demonstrates that identifying symptoms and obtaining care are different stages.

### Relevance to Learms
Resource discovery, appointment readiness, privacy explanation and human handoff are distinct features. A checklist completion must not be reported as care received.

### Citation
Rotenstein LS et al. (2016). *JAMA*, 316(21), 2214–2236. doi:10.1001/jama.2016.17324.

## Topic
Stigma can impede help-seeking

### Source
Clement et al. systematic review of 144 studies with 90,189 participants; Bannatyne et al. systematic review of self-stigma interventions in medical students/doctors. [MED-103, MED-109]

### Key Finding
Clement et al. found a small-to-moderate negative relationship between stigma and help-seeking in mixed populations, with disclosure concerns prominent. Bannatyne et al. found only five eligible intervention publications, none designed explicitly around self-stigma; three reported improvement, with limited follow-up and moderate-quality evidence. General-population associations and small intervention literatures must not be represented as Pakistan-specific effects.

### Relevance to Learms
Use non-stigmatizing labels, allow private browsing without profile disclosure, and do not promise that a short educational module will change help-seeking behavior.

### Citation
Clement S et al. (2015). What is the impact of mental health-related stigma on help-seeking? *Psychological Medicine*, 45(1), 11–27. doi:10.1017/S0033291714000129. Bannatyne AJ et al. (2023). *Frontiers in Medicine*, 10:1204274. doi:10.3389/fmed.2023.1204274.

## Topic
Pakistan students described distrust and access barriers

### Source
Khurshid et al. qualitative focus group. [MED-095]

### Key Finding
Participants described services as absent, poorly advertised, difficult to access or distrusted because of confidentiality concerns. A single purposive focus group cannot estimate service availability or trust nationally, but it identifies testable implementation risks.

### Relevance to Learms
For every listed service, show who operates it, whether it is institution-affiliated, what data are shared, hours, cost, eligibility and verification date. “Confidential” requires a source-backed definition.

### Citation
Khurshid S, Khurshid S, Toor HK. (2025). *BMC Medical Education*, 25, 1190. doi:10.1186/s12909-025-07762-y.

## Topic
Digital support does not replace acute-risk assessment

### Source
WHO 2023 conditional recommendation on digital interventions for suicidal thoughts. [MED-104]

### Key Finding
WHO's recommendation is conditional with low certainty and concerns evidence-based intervention content. It does not authorize a general education platform or generative model to assess, monitor or manage acute suicide risk.

### Relevance to Learms
No automated reassurance, diagnosis, triage score, therapeutic claim, or silent risk monitoring. A distress route should make human and emergency options immediately visible, permit instant exit, avoid engagement mechanics, and be reviewed by qualified clinical, privacy and local legal stakeholders.

### Citation
World Health Organization (2023). *Digital interventions: mhGAP Evidence Centre—self-harm and suicide*. Official WHO page, accessed 2026-09-30.

## Topic
Harassment support and mental-health support are separate routes

### Source
Pakistan mistreatment studies and HEC revised harassment policy. [MED-097, MED-098, MED-105]

### Key Finding
Harassment can require safety, procedural, legal and institutional responses regardless of whether a student reports distress. HEC defines a formal inquiry route; counselling does not replace that route, and an inquiry committee is not a treatment provider.

### Relevance to Learms
Do not funnel all reports into a wellbeing chatbot. Present distinct choices: learn about policy, find confidential advice, contact the inquiry mechanism, seek independent support, or access wellbeing care.

### Citation
Ahmer et al. (2008), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0003889. Shoukat et al. (2010), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0013429. HEC Pakistan, revised harassment policy, accessed 2026-09-30.
