## 1. Pakistan's media industry is contracting, and students have noticed

### Topic: The Pakistani media crisis and its effect on journalism education
### Source: Bakar, M. A., Yousaf, Z. & Ali, A. (2024). "Unraveling the Media Crisis in Pakistan: A Qualitative Analysis of the Root Causes of Media Layoffs and Closures." *Global Regional Review* IX:124–132. · Deutsche Welle (May 2024), "Pakistan: Students switch off from media careers" · Committee to Protect Journalists
### Key Finding **[HIGH — peer-reviewed qualitative study + reporting with named HEC/editor sources]**

**The demand signal has already collapsed at the education layer:**
- **Journalism schools in Pakistan report low enrolments.**
- **Several universities in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa have shelved their journalism programmes** due to
  declining demand, and **at least five more are at risk**.
- At least one university attempted to **merge media departments to cut costs** (reversed after
  student protest).
- Students are switching out: *"I took up a journalism graduate course but soon switched to a
  business program for a better future because both mainstream newspapers and news channels had
  limited openings."* — former AIOU student, quoted by DW.

**Why (three causes, from the peer-reviewed study):**
1. **Recession / advertising collapse.** Reduced ad revenue plus macroeconomic contraction
   threatened outlet viability.
2. **Fallout with governments** — political pressure, plus self-censorship and alignment with
   owners' political preferences constraining journalistic function.
3. **Technological disruption.** Pakistani legacy media were "relatively less responsive to the
   digital revolution"; no funds were made available to upgrade technology **or to train
   journalists for the digital shift**, leaving them beaten by "faster, leaner" digital-first rivals.
4. Plus **financial mismanagement** within media organisations (respondent theme).

**Working conditions:**
- **"Many channels are reluctant to pay salaries to their employees even after the 6th month,
  which has resulted in severe mental stress, traumas, and psychological issues."** (peer-reviewed
  study, verbatim). Six months of unpaid salary is a documented norm, not an anecdote.
- Low salaries, delayed payments, and job insecurity are reported industry-wide [DW].
- **64 journalists killed in connection with their work since 1992** [Committee to Protect
  Journalists]. Physical safety is a real, quantified feature of this career.

**HEC's own diagnosis** (Dr Mukhtar Ahmad, HEC Chairman, to DW): during the 2000s electronic-media
boom "many youths jumped on the news channel bandwagon without considering future prospects. The
popularity of digital media burst the news TV 'bubble' causing large-scale layoffs and massive pay
cuts, which disillusioned students with the profession of journalism." Aamir Ghauri (editor,
*The News International*) adds that the economy could not support the number of outlets that opened.

**International corroboration:** in the UK, HESA Graduate Outcomes data shows Media/Journalism/
Communications graduates with elevated unemployment 15 months out, driven by newsroom cuts,
audience decline and platform transformation, with new skill expectations (social video, digital
editing, data-led storytelling) creating "harsh competition for fewer early-career roles."
Creative industries were the slowest sector to recover from the pandemic. So this is a **global
structural shift**, not only a Pakistani failure.

## 2. What this means for Learms — a small field with a sharp, specific need

1. **Do not build a traditional journalism curriculum.** The degrees themselves are closing. The
   market has voted.
2. **Build the skills the industry says are missing.** The peer-reviewed study identifies the exact
   gap: *no funds were made available to train journalists for the digital shift*. The needed skills
   are enumerable and teachable online:
   - digital/mobile video production and editing
   - social-first storytelling and platform-native formats
   - **data journalism** (spreadsheets → analysis → visualisation)
   - SEO, audience analytics, newsletter/creator economics
   - fact-checking and verification (OSINT basics)
   - podcast production
   - **content production in Urdu for digital platforms** — the growth area
3. **The real destination is the creator economy and digital marketing, not newsrooms.** A media
   graduate in Pakistan is far more likely to earn from freelance content, brand social media,
   agency work or YouTube than from a TV channel. Learms' media track should be honest about that
   and teach it.
4. **Media students are a natural bridge to Learms' own content production** — a plausible
   contributor/creator pipeline.
5. **Safety and ethics content is not optional** given 64 journalist deaths since 1992 and
   documented self-censorship pressure. If Learms teaches journalism at all, it must teach digital
   security and source protection. **Handle with care and expert review; this is a politically
   sensitive area in Pakistan.**
6. **Unpaid-salary reality (6+ months) means media students have essentially no ability to pay.**
   Free tier only for this vertical.
