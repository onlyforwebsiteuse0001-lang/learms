## 1. The sector they are training into is in structural decline

### Topic: State of Pakistani agriculture
### Source: Pakistan Economic Survey 2025-26 (as analysed by *The Friday Times*, Jun 2026); IndraStra analysis of the Economic Survey (Sep 2026); Concave Agri (2025); Foreign Policy (2022); The Guardian (2022); World Bank / Atlantic Council flood damage estimates
### Key Finding **[HIGH — government Survey data, multiple independent readings]**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Agriculture share of GDP | **23.44%** (down from 23.62%) |
| Share of workforce employed in agriculture | **~37%**; supports livelihoods of **>100 million** people |
| Livestock share of agricultural value added | **62.45%** (grew 3.75%) |
| **"Important crops" growth** | **+0.65%** — near-stagnant |
| Agriculture growth FY2024-25 | **0.56%**, with **major crops contracting 13.5%** |
| Wheat 2024-25 | 29.61m tonnes — **but many growers sold below cost of production** |
| Irrigation efficiency | **below 45%**; **35–40% of water lost** before reaching crops |
| Agriculture's share of freshwater withdrawal | **>90%** |
| Canal water losses (Jan 2025) | **38%** (seepage + theft) |
| Post-harvest losses, fruit & veg | **20–40%** |
| Agriculture's share of national GHG emissions | **~40%** |
| Temperature rise to date | **+0.5–1.0 °C** over recent decades; projected **+3–5 °C** by 2100 |
| National poverty | approaching **29%** |

**Climate shocks, quantified:**
- **2022 floods:** >33 million people affected; **>4 million acres of crops damaged**; ~80% of crops
  destroyed in Sindh; 70% in parts of Balochistan; total damages/losses **$30–35 billion**
  (World Bank); agriculture damage **$3.18bn** and livestock **$291m** (Atlantic Council); food
  inflation hit **36%** y/y; seed prices up ~30%.
- **2025 floods:** damages **PKR 822 billion (~$2.9bn)**, of which **PKR 430bn agriculture**
  (PKR 422.6bn crop loss); >1,039 fatalities; >4m displaced; ~6.5m affected across 70 districts,
  **77% concentrated in Punjab**; projected **+0.2 million unemployment in FY2026**.
- Pakistan emits **<1% of global GHG** (historic share ~0.4%) while absorbing these impacts.
- Groundwater tables falling; **solar tube wells lower energy cost but accelerate depletion** — a
  genuinely under-discussed perverse incentive.

### Relevance to Learms
1. **Agriculture graduates have 29.4% unemployment** (PIDE, 2020-21 — the *highest* of any
   disaggregated discipline, up from 11.4%). Combine that with a stagnant crop sector and this is
   the field where "study hard, get a job" is least true. Learms' value here is **skills and
   entrepreneurship**, not credentials.
2. **Livestock is where the growth is (62% of value added, +3.75%) while crops stagnate (+0.65%).**
   Content and career guidance should weight toward **livestock, dairy, poultry and veterinary**,
   not traditional agronomy. This is a concrete, data-backed content-prioritisation decision that
   contradicts the way Pakistani agriculture curricula are weighted.
3. **The named, teachable problem set is unusually concrete**: irrigation efficiency (<45%),
   post-harvest losses (20–40%), climate-smart agriculture, crop insurance, weather forecasting,
   soil health, market linkage. These are *practical modules*, each tied to a quantified national
   loss figure — excellent, motivating content design.
4. **Agriculture students often come from farming families in rural districts** — the segment with
   the worst connectivity and the lowest ability to pay. **Offline-first is non-negotiable for this
   vertical** (see `PAKISTAN_SOLUTIONS.md`).
5. **Extension is the killer app.** Pakistan's agricultural extension system is weak; an agriculture
   *student* with a phone could serve as a local extension agent. A "student → farmer advisory"
   feature is speculative but strategically interesting. **[Hypothesis, not researched.]**

## 2. Fieldwork, market instability, and study conditions

### Key Finding **[MED — inference from verified sector data; no student-level study found]**
- **Market instability is documented at farm level**: 2024-25 wheat sold below cost of production;
  labour costs "more than doubled in many areas while harvest prices failed to keep pace"; fertiliser
  and pesticide costs rising. A student from a farming household is therefore facing *household
  income shocks during their degree* — a direct financial-stress pathway (see `SUCCESS_FINANCIAL.md`).
- **Fieldwork is weather- and season-bound**, and flood/heatwave events can destroy a whole
  practical season. Unlike a lab, it cannot be rescheduled.
- HEC revised the **Agriculture curriculum in 2025-26** (the most recent cohort) — so content built
  against it is currently fresh.

### Gaps / Not found
- **No study of Pakistani agriculture/veterinary students** specifically (motivation, dropout,
  fieldwork barriers) was located. This section is therefore *inference from sector data*, clearly
  the weakest evidential basis in the Area-2 set.
- No DVM-specific data (PVMC registration numbers, pass rates).
- No data on rural connectivity among agriculture students.
