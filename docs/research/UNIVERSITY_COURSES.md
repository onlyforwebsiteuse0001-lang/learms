## 1. Market size — how many students are actually out there?

### Topic: Higher-education enrolment in Pakistan
### Source: HEC Annual Report 2024-25; Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25 (Ministry of Finance); Economic Survey 2025 as reported by Momentum Pakistan & Bloom Pakistan (June 2026)
### Key Finding — **[HIGH confidence on structure, [CONTESTED] on the exact number]**

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Universities / DAIs | **269** (Public 160, Private 109) | Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25, FY2024 |
| University teachers | 58,814 | Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25 |
| HEIs coordinated by HEC for data | 271 | HEC Annual Report 2024-25 |
| **University enrolment** | **2,081,654** (provisional, 2023-24) | HEC Annual Report 2024-25 |
| Female participation | **47%** of university enrolment | HEC Annual Report 2024-25 |
| Graduates per year | **571,148** | HEC Annual Report 2024-25 |
| PhDs registered/year | 3,317 | HEC Annual Report 2024-25 |
| Degree colleges | ~2,516–2,573, ~0.65m students | Economic Survey, FY2024 |
| Higher-secondary (Grade 11-12) | ~2.5m students | Economic Survey (2021-23 basis) |
| Secondary (Grade 9-10) | ~4.5m students | Economic Survey |
| Technical & vocational | ~455,000 | Economic Survey |
| Education spend | **0.8% of GDP** | Economic Survey 2024-25 |

### ⚠ The most important market finding: **enrolment is FALLING**

- 2014-15: **1.29–1.30 million**
- 2020-21: **2.23 million** (peak)
- 2022-23: **1.94 million**
- 2024-25: **1.96 million** (slight recovery)
- **Net: −11.8%, roughly −262,000 students from peak**, attributed to inflation and rising
  education costs. [Pakistan Economic Survey 2025, reported by Momentum Pakistan & Bloom Pakistan, 24 Jun 2026]

**[CONTESTED]** HEC's Annual Report 2024-25 reports **2.08m** for 2023-24 and frames it as growth
("more than doubled over the past decade, from 1.29m in 2014-15 to 2.08m in 2023-24"), while the
Economic Survey series shows a decline from a 2.23m peak. The two series are probably counting
different populations (HEC's 271 coordinated HEIs vs the Survey's universities-only series) and
different years. **Do not cite a single headline number.** Safe statement: *"roughly 2 million
university students in Pakistan, down from a 2020-21 peak."*

### Relevance to Learms — this changes strategy, not just a slide
1. **The market is price-constrained, not attention-constrained.** Students are leaving university
   *because of cost*. A product priced like a Western edtech SaaS will fail. Free tier + very low
   PKR price point is not a nice-to-have, it is the whole go-to-market. See `PAKISTAN_SOLUTIONS.md`.
2. **The pre-university funnel is 3–4× bigger than the university funnel**: ~4.5m in Grade 9-10 plus
   ~2.5m in Grade 11-12 vs ~2m in universities. Reinforces Taxonomy §4 recommendation to treat
   `pre-university` as a first-class field.
3. **47% female participation** at university level is high and rising — the product must be
   comfortable and safe for women users (privacy settings, no forced public profiles, no
   location sharing, moderated peer features). This is a design constraint, not a marketing point.
4. **571k graduates/year** is the ceiling on the "fresh graduate / job-readiness" segment.
5. **0.8% of GDP on education** ⇒ institutions will not buy expensive B2B licences at scale.
   B2C freemium and low-cost B2B2C (via colleges/academies) are the plausible channels.

### Source: QS World University Rankings 2026 / QS Asia 2026; HEC national rankings; aggregator syntheses (2026)
### Key Finding

**[CONTESTED — rankings differ by source and edition.]** QS global positions reported for QAU range
from #354 to ~#378 across aggregators for the same 2026 edition, and NUST from #334–400 to #371.
Aggregators are unreliable at this precision. What is *robust* across all sources:

- **17 Pakistani universities** appear in QS World University Rankings 2026. [clicks.com.pk, 2026]
- **8 Pakistani universities** appear in QS Asia 2026: NUST #68, QAU #89, LUMS #129, COMSATS #150, … [TechJuice, Nov 2025]
- QAU and NUST contest #1 nationally (QAU on research output, NUST on engineering + global brand).

| Tier | Institutions | Why Learms cares |

## 3. What a Pakistani "course" actually looks like (data model implications)

Derived from the HEC Undergraduate Education Policy V1.1 and the 2024-26 curriculum booklets
(see `PAKISTAN_FIELDS_TAXONOMY.md` for citations).

### 3.1 Course notation
Pakistani schemes of study write credit hours as **`3(3-0)`** or **`3(2+1)`**:
- First number = total credit hours
- `(3-0)` = 3 hours theory, 0 lab
- `(2+1)` = 2 theory + 1 lab (a 1-credit lab is typically a 3-hour weekly practical)

**Learms must parse and store this.** A `Course` needs
`{ code, title, credit_hours_total, theory_cr, lab_cr, category }`.

### 3.2 Course categories used by HEC
`Major: Core` · `Major: Elective` · `Interdisciplinary / Allied (IDS-I..IV)` · `General Education` ·
`Field Experience / Internship` · `Capstone Project`

These are the *actual enum values* in HEC curriculum tables — use them verbatim.

### 3.3 Typical 4-year BS shape
| Component | Credit hours |
|---|---|
| General Education | 30 (12 courses) |
| Major (core + elective) | 72–87 |
| Interdisciplinary / allied | ≥12 |
| Field experience / internship | 3–6 |
| Capstone project | 3–6 |
| **Total** | **122–140** |

Specialisation nomenclature rule (from HEC's 2025 Political Science booklet): to award a degree
*with a named specialisation*, the department must offer **≥6 electives = 18 credit hours,
i.e. ≥25% of the 72-credit major**. This is a concrete rule Learms can use to define
"specialisation tracks" in its own content structure.

### 3.4 Grading
- Semester system with **CGPA out of 4.0** at most universities; annual-system colleges still use
  divisions (First/Second Division).
- HEC's own PhD entry rule references *both* "CGPA 3.0/4.0 (semester)" **or** "First Division (annual)"
  — confirming both systems coexist nationally. Learms' progress model must support **both**.

### 3.5 Assessment convention
Typical split (varies by university, verify per institution): sessional/internal ~25–30%
(quizzes, assignments, attendance), **midterm ~20–30%**, **final ~40–50%**. This shape matters:
Learms' study planner should default to **two high-stakes peaks per semester (week ~8 and week ~16)**
plus continuous low-stakes assessment — which happens to align perfectly with spaced retrieval.

## 4. Course catalogue coverage — what was and wasn't obtained

### Obtained (primary source, machine-extractable)
HEC publishes full schemes of studies as PDFs for **~50 disciplines** revised 2015-16 → 2025-26.
These PDFs contain semester-by-semester tables with course titles, codes, credit hours and
category. They are the **single best bulk source of Pakistani course structure data in existence**
and they are free.

Most recently revised (2024-26), i.e. the highest-value scrape targets:
Fine Arts · Computer Science · Business & Management Studies · Pakistan Studies · Bioinformatics ·
Geography · Allied Health Sciences · Cyber Security · Agriculture · Biochemistry · Physical Education ·
Microbiology · Architecture · Physics · Political Science · Zoology · Sociology · Psychology ·
Chemistry · Islamic Studies · Remote Sensing & GIS · Public Health · Mathematics · International
Relations · Media & Communication Studies · Commerce · Geology · Home Economics · Education ·
Paediatric Nursing · DPT · Statistics · Biology · Environmental Science · English · Social Work ·
Library & Information Science · History · LLB · Pharm.D · Food Science & Technology ·
Human Nutrition & Dietetics · Nursing (BSN/MSN) · Midwifery · Biotechnology · Botany ·
Computing Disciplines.

Index page: https://www.hec.gov.pk/english/services/universities/RevisedCurricula/Pages/default.aspx

### Not obtained — **Gap**
- Individual university course catalogues for the "top 50" were **not** individually scraped in this
  pass. Universities publish schemes of study inconsistently (some as PDFs behind portals, some only
  in prospectuses). **This is a real gap.**
- **Recommendation:** do *not* attempt to model 50 universities' catalogues. Model the **HEC
  curriculum as the canonical spine**, and treat university-specific deviations as *overrides*
  (a `university_course_override` table). Universities are required to align with HEC curricula
  anyway; deviations are typically elective choices and course codes, not content.
