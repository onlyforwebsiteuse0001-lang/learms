## 1. The system they are training into

### Topic: Scale and state of Pakistani schooling
### Source: **Pakistan Education Statistics 2021-22** (PIE, Ministry of Federal Education — primary); *Girls' Education Statistics and Trends Report 2023-24* (Federal Ministry of Education, via Dawn, Feb 2026); Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25; ASER 2023; Pakistan Observer report (Jan 2026)
### Key Finding **[HIGH — government primary sources]**

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Educational institutions (all types/levels) | **313,418** (227,506 = 73% formal schools) | PES 2021-22 |
| **Out-of-school children** | **26.2 million** = **39%** of school-age children | PES 2021-22 |
| OOSC trend | 44% (2016-17) → 39% (2021-22) **but absolute number ROSE** from 22.02m to 26.21m | PES 2021-22 |
| Teachers (all levels) | ~**1.83 million** | via Friday Times, 2025 |
| Teachers with satisfactory subject knowledge (public schools) | **only 40%** | ASER 2023 |
| Education budget share (national) | fell **13% → 11%** | Girls' Ed Report 2023-24 |
| **Share of education budget consumed by salaries** | **94%** | Girls' Ed Report 2023-24 |
| Schools with digital tools | **19%** | Girls' Ed Report 2023-24 |
| Schools with disability ramps | 23% (assistive materials "scarce") | Girls' Ed Report 2023-24 |
| Human Capital Index | **0.41** — a child born today will realise 41% of potential productivity | via Pakistan Observer, 2026 |
| Youth population | ~140 million | Federal Ministry of Education |

⚠ **[CONTESTED] Education spending as % of GDP.** Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25 Ch.10 states
**0.8% of GDP**; the Pakistan Observer report states **1.9%**. These almost certainly measure
different things (federal-only vs federal+provincial, or different years). **Never quote a single
figure without saying which source.** Both agree the number is far below the internationally
recommended **4–6%**.

### Relevance to Learms
- **94% of the education budget goes to salaries.** There is essentially *no* institutional budget
  for digital learning tools in Pakistani public schools. **B2G and B2B school sales are structurally
  near-impossible.** This is a hard commercial finding: go **B2C**, or via private schools/academies,
  or via free-tier + NGO/donor funding. Do not build an enterprise sales motion for public schools.
- **Only 19% of schools have digital tools** — so a school-dependent distribution strategy also fails.
  Reach students through **their own phones**, not their schools.
- **26.2 million out-of-school children** is not Learms' addressable market (they lack devices,
  literacy and money) but it defines the *social* context: any "Pakistan's education crisis" framing
  must acknowledge that the deepest problem is access, which an app does not solve.

## 2. Teacher quality and training

### Key Finding **[HIGH on the numbers, MED on causal claims]**
- **Only 40% of public-school teachers have satisfactory subject-specific knowledge** [ASER 2023].
  The majority rely on rote-focused methods.
- **Pakistan Education Statistics 2018-19** found a large number of teachers, **especially rural**,
  remain *undertrained and ill-equipped*.
- **~65% of English teachers lack professional ELT pedagogical training** [British Council 2021].
- **National Education Policy 2009** and **2017** both emphasised teacher quality and CPD; "no
  practical implementation has ever been witnessed." The **Teacher Development Programme (TDP)**
  under the Education Sector Plan 2019-2025 has had limited impact due to underfunding.
- The National Policy Framework **lacks a strategy for teacher recruitment, training and CPD**
  altogether, despite SDG 4.c.
- "Studies indicate **low engagement with continuous learning among teachers**, undermining classroom
  outcomes. Without strengthening the quality of educators, improvements in curricula or funding are
  unlikely to yield results."
- Positive counter-example: **Teach for Pakistan** recruits fresh graduates, trains them and places
  them in under-resourced schools, with reported gains in engagement and performance. **[LOW —
  program self-report; no independent evaluation found.]**

### Relevance to Learms — the teacher is a *user*, not an obstacle
This is the most under-exploited insight in the whole Area-2 research:

1. **Teachers are a distinct, high-leverage user segment.** 1.83 million of them, 60% with
   inadequate subject knowledge, no CPD system, and a government policy vacuum. A teacher who uses
   Learms to prepare a lesson reaches 30–100 students. **The unit economics of a teacher user are
   30–100× a student user.**
2. HEC's **Education 2025 curriculum already contains "Educational Technology", "Online and Distance
   Learning" and "Artificial Intelligence in Education" as named B.Ed specialisations**
   (see `PAKISTAN_FIELDS_TAXONOMY.md` §2.7) — so there is an *official curriculum slot* for
   exactly the content Learms is best placed to produce.
3. Concrete teacher features: lesson-plan generation aligned to the board syllabus, past-paper item
   generation, a subject-knowledge refresher track, marking assistance, and Urdu-medium explanations
   of concepts the teacher themself is shaky on (offered **privately**, without shaming).
4. **Dignity is a design constraint.** A product that implies "your teacher doesn't know this" will
   be rejected by teachers and by the culture. Frame as *support*, never as *replacement* or *audit*.

## 3. Teacher shortage, burnout and engagement

### Key Finding **[MED]**
- Teacher shortage is widely reported as a pressing structural problem, driven by inadequate
  recruitment, low pay and rural posting difficulty.
- "Low engagement with continuous learning among teachers" is documented.
- **Gap:** no rigorous Pakistan-specific study of **teacher burnout prevalence** was found in this
  pass. International teacher-burnout literature (Maslach Burnout Inventory-Educators Survey) exists
  but was not researched. Marked NOT FOUND — this was an explicit brief item and remains open.

## 4. Outdated pedagogy and low engagement

### Key Finding **[HIGH]**
Covered in depth in `PROBLEMS_BUSINESS.md` §2. Summary as it applies to teacher education:
- **4 of 5 students rote-memorise to pass** [UNDP Pakistan NHDR].
- 10 years of Grade 10/12 English board papers contained **hardly any analytical or creative-level
  questions** [Rind et al., 2019].
- Only items expected on the exam get taught; grammar-translation method dominates
  [Summro & Shah, 2016].
- Curricula offer "limited exposure to digital skills, critical thinking and applied learning."
- **64% of graduates face employment difficulties due to skill gaps**; **58% of employers report
  difficulty finding appropriate workers**; graduate youth unemployment ~**31%**.
  [Pakistan Observer report, 2026 — **[MED]**, survey methodology not published]

### Relevance to Learms
Teacher-education students are simultaneously (a) victims of rote pedagogy and (b) about to
propagate it. Content that teaches **how to write a higher-order question**, **how to run
retrieval practice in a 60-student class with no equipment**, and **how to give formative feedback**
is cheap to produce and has a multiplier effect. Pair it with the evidence base in
`SOLUTIONS_BY_CATEGORY.md` (Dunlosky's practice testing / distributed practice ratings).
