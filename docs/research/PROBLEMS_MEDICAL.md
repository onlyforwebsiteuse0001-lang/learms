## 1. The global baseline — Rotenstein et al. (2016), the reference meta-analysis

### Topic: Depression and suicidal ideation among medical students worldwide
### Source: Rotenstein, L. S., Ramos, M. A., Torre, M., Segal, J. B., Peluso, M. J., Guille, C., Sen, S., Mata, D. A. (2016). "Prevalence of Depression, Depressive Symptoms, and Suicidal Ideation Among Medical Students: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *JAMA* 316(21):2214–2236. doi:10.1001/jama.2016.17324
### Key Finding **[HIGH — 195 studies, 129,123 students, 47 countries]**

| Outcome | Pooled prevalence | Range | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depression / depressive symptoms** | **27.2%** (95% CI 24.7–29.9) | 9.3% – 55.9% | 37,933 / 122,356 |
| **Suicidal ideation** | **11.1%** | 7.4% – 24.2% | — |
| **Sought psychiatric treatment** (among those screening positive) | **15.7%** | — | — |

Critical secondary findings:
- **No significant difference between pre-clinical and clinical students** (23.7% vs 22.4%, P = .72).
  The problem is not "the wards"; it starts early and persists.
- **Only ~1 in 6 depressed medical students seeks treatment.** A related meta-analysis puts it even
  lower at **12.9%**. This help-seeking gap is the single most actionable fact in this document.
- Extreme between-study heterogeneity (I² very high) — the 27.2% is a *central tendency across very
  different contexts*, not a universal rate.

Adjacent pooled estimates from the surrounding literature:
- **Anxiety ≈ 33.8%** among medical students [Quek et al. / Puthran et al. lineage]
- **Burnout ≈ 44.2%** pooled; some cohort reviews report up to **88%**
- A large single-university study (n = 1,925, Paris Cité) found burnout in **42% of non-clinical**
  and **65% of clinical students/residents**, 7-day anxiety symptoms 55%, 12-month suicidal
  thoughts 19%.

⚠ **[CONTESTED]** Whether medical students are worse off than *matched peers* is genuinely disputed.
Several studies find no difference in depression, suicidal ideation or anxiety versus humanities,
nursing, pharmacy or business students; one meta-analysis explicitly reported "no significant
difference in prevalences of depression between medical and non-medical students." **Do not claim
"medicine is uniquely damaging."** The defensible claim is: *prevalence is high in absolute terms
and treatment-seeking is very low.*

### Citation
- https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2589340
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9378210 (Paris Cité multi-faculty study)
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299253400_Prevalence_of_depression_amongst_medical_students_A_meta-analysis

## 2. Pakistan — the rates are roughly **double** the global pooled estimate

### Topic: Mental distress among Pakistani medical undergraduates
### Source: Systematic review of Pakistani medical colleges 2004–2019 (*J Pak Med Assoc*); Azim & Baig (2019) *JPMA*, Liaquat National Medical College Karachi; Arif et al. (2021) *PJMHS*, CMH Lahore; Siddiqui et al. (2026 preprint, Research Square) — 9 Islamabad medical colleges; various Karachi studies
### Key Finding **[HIGH on "much higher than global"; MED on any specific number]**

| Study | Setting | n | Depression | Anxiety | Stress |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Siddiqui et al. (2026 preprint)** | **9 medical colleges, Islamabad** | **1,630** | **57.8%** (PHQ-9 ≥10) | **46.4%** (GAD-7 ≥10) | — |
| Azim & Baig (2019), JPMA | Liaquat National MC, Karachi | 188 | **71%** | **72%** | 35% |
| Systematic review 2004–2019 | Pakistani medical colleges (18 studies) | — | **range 20% – 90%** | 44%+ | — |
| Sheikh et al. (in review) | Pakistan | — | **90%** (highest reported) | — | — |
| Islamabad DASS study | Islamabad | — | — | **74.2%** | **50%** |
| Arif et al. (2021), CMH Lahore | students/interns/residents | 104 | 26.9% *extremely severe* | 34.6% *extremely severe* | 12.5% severe |
| Karachi private MC (2018) | Karachi | — | — | all students scored >19 on AKUADS | — |

**Synthesis: Pakistani medical-student depression prevalence clusters around 57–71%, versus a
global pooled 27.2%.** Even the most conservative Pakistani multi-centre study (n=1,630) is
**>2× the global figure**. The 90% outlier should be treated as a measurement artefact, not a fact.

**Identified predictors and causes (Pakistan-specific, from the studies above):**
1. **Academic burden and examinations** — named as the dominant stressor in essentially every study.
2. **Peer pressure**, **poor sleep**, and **screen time** — the three strongest modifiable predictors
   in the 1,630-student multivariable model [Siddiqui et al., 2026].
3. **Family expectations** — "pressure of living up to family's expectations" is a *recurring,
   distinctly South-Asian stressor* named in Karachi focus groups. Related finding: **students who
   chose medicine under family influence were more likely to report suicidal ideation.**
4. **Financial constraints**, and the out-of-pocket healthcare financing model, which **delays
   help-seeking**.
5. **Year-of-study effects**: distress peaks in **1st and 3rd year** — the two transition points
   (entry, and entry into clinical rotations). 3rd-years reported losing their friendship group and
   rotating through unfamiliar teams. Anxiety/stress were generally higher in years 3, 4, 5.
6. **Lack of support services** for stress management, and lack of physical/recreational facilities.
7. **Religion was the most commonly adopted coping strategy** — a crucial, non-obvious design input.

⚠ **Methodological caveats (must be stated honestly):** most Pakistani studies are single-centre,
cross-sectional, convenience-sampled, and use screening instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, DASS-21, AKUADS)
that measure *symptoms*, not clinical diagnoses. Screening cut-offs over-estimate clinical
prevalence. The 20–90% range across 18 studies is itself evidence of instrument and sampling
inconsistency. **Correct phrasing: "over half of Pakistani medical students screen positive for
depressive symptoms."**

### Citation
- https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9666691/v1.pdf (Siddiqui et al., n=1,630, Islamabad)
- https://www.archive.jpma.org.pk/article-details/9195. (Azim & Baig 2019, full text)
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364127451_jpma_systematic_review (2004–2019 systematic review)

## 3. Study-pressure structure specific to Pakistani medicine

| Pressure source | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **MDCAT gatekeeping** | 180 MCQs, one shot per year, province-wise; merit cut-offs far above the 55% floor; FSc eligibility raised to 65% for 2025-26. [PM&DC 2025] |
| **5-year MBBS, min 160 credit hours** | HEC licensure-degree rule. |
| **Volume + recall** | MDCAT is 70% recall; MBBS professional exams continue the pattern. |
| **Transition shocks** | Year 1 (entry) and Year 3 (clinical entry) are the empirically-identified distress peaks. |
| **Family-chosen careers** | Associated with elevated suicidal ideation. |
| **Cost** | Private MBBS fees are a major household expense; out-of-pocket health spending delays care. |

## 4. Sleep deprivation

### Key Finding **[MED]**
- **Poor sleep is one of the three strongest statistical predictors of depression and anxiety** in
  the largest Pakistani sample (n = 1,630) [Siddiqui et al., 2026], alongside peer pressure and
  screen time.
- Sleep disturbance is listed as "highly prevalent" in Pakistani medical-student studies generally.
- **Gap:** no Pakistani study with objective sleep measurement (actigraphy) was found; all is
  self-report.

### Relevance to Learms
Sleep and screen time are **the two predictors Learms directly influences** — and Learms is itself
a screen. A study app that encourages 2 a.m. cramming is actively pushing on two of the three
measured risk factors. This is an ethical obligation, not a feature idea:
- Do **not** ship streak mechanics that punish a missed day (they drive late-night compliance).
  See the gamification critique in `UI_VISUAL_DESIGN.md` and `TECH_SOLUTIONS.md`.
- Ship a **default quiet window** (e.g. no notifications 23:00–07:00, user-adjustable).
- Ship **session-length nudges** and an end-of-session stop signal rather than infinite scroll.
- FSRS scheduling should *avoid* stacking large review loads the night before an exam — spread
  earlier by design.

## 5. Clinical-skills gap

### Key Finding **[MED — inferred from verified structural sources, not a dedicated Pakistani study]**
- The Rotenstein finding that pre-clinical and clinical distress are equal (23.7% vs 22.4%)
  undermines the assumption that clinical exposure is the main stressor — yet Pakistani focus groups
  identify **year 3 (start of clinical rotations)** as a distress peak. The reconcilable
  interpretation: it is the **transition**, not the clinical work, that hurts.
- HEC's 2025-26 revision created a dedicated **Allied Health Sciences** BS curriculum covering
  Medical Lab Technology, Radiology, Anaesthesia Tech, OT Tech, Optometry, etc. — a formal
  acknowledgement that skills-based health training was previously unstandardised.

### Relevance to Learms
- Build explicit **transition-support content** at two moments: (a) MBBS Year 1 onboarding,
  (b) the pre-clinical→clinical transition. These are the empirically identified break-points.
- Clinical reasoning is a **case-based, Socratic** skill — the opposite of MDCAT's recall regime.
  Learms must switch pedagogy modes when a user moves from MDCAT prep to MBBS clinical years:
  vignette → differential → investigation → management, with hint-laddering. See `SOCRATIC_DEEP.md`.

## Gaps / Not found
- No Pakistani study using objective (non-self-report) sleep measures.
- No Pakistani longitudinal study tracking the same cohort across years.
- No data on Pakistani medical students' actual use of counselling services (only that services are
  perceived as lacking).
- No evaluation of any digital mental-health intervention with Pakistani medical students.
