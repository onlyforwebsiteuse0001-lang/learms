# Wellbeing and Retention Papers — Agent 5

- Status: Draft v0.1, Area 11 in progress
- Source anchors: S088-S091

## Topic: CS attrition is a documented concern
### Source: Beaubouef and Mason, 2005
### Key Finding: The paper asks why attrition in CS is high and discusses contributing issues.
### Relevance to Learms: Learms should monitor early frustration signals and offer recovery paths.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1145/1083431.1083474

## Topic: Impostor phenomenon affects CS students
### Source: Rosenstein, Raghu, and Porter, 2020
### Key Finding: The paper identifies impostor phenomenon prevalence among CS students.
### Relevance to Learms: Avoid shame-based rankings; show growth and normalize bugs.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1145/3328778.3366815

## Topic: Impostor syndrome may relate to code comprehension outcomes
### Source: Chen et al., 2024
### Key Finding: The exploratory study examines final-year CS students using code comprehension tasks and biometrics/eye-tracking in relation to impostor syndrome.
### Relevance to Learms: Learner confidence and comprehension should be tracked sensitively.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-61569-6_2

## Topic: Choice overload is relevant but not IT-specific
### Source: Iyengar and Lepper, 2000
### Key Finding: In some contexts, extensive choice can reduce motivation/action; later literature has nuance, and this is not IT-specific.
### Relevance to Learms: Use choice-overload as a caution for onboarding/path UI, not as a definitive computing claim.
### Citation: https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995

## Product Implications

| Risk | Product Response |
|---|---|
| early failure | diagnostics and recovery plans |
| impostor thoughts | private mastery evidence and supportive copy |
| path overload | guided recommender and limited first choices |
| dropout | next achievable milestone and mentor/peer prompts |
